        OF
Captain <i>William Fly</i>,
    And his CREW.

As to the Birth of this Pyrate, we can discover nothing by the Enquiries we have hitherto made; and, indeed, had we succeeded in our Search, could it have been of any great Consequence? For, its certain, by the Behaviour of the Man, he must have been of very obscure Parents; and, by his Education, (as he was no Artist) very unfit, in all Respects, except that of Cruelty, for the villainous Business he was in.We have been inform'd, that he had been a Pyrate in a private Capacity, and having escaped Justice, had an Opportunity of repenting his former Crimes, and, as a foremast Man, or petty Officer, of getting his Bread in a warrantable Way: But no; ignorant as he was of Letters, he was ambitious of Power, and capable of the most barbarous Actions to acquire it.

Captain <i>Green</i> of <i>Bristol</i>, in <i>April</i> 1726, shipp'd this <i>Fly</i> as Boatswain, at <i>Jamaica</i>, being bound, in the <i>Elizabeth</i> Snow of <i>Bristol</i>, for the Coast of <i>Guinea</i>. <i>Fly</i>, who had insinuated himself with some of the Men, whom he found ripe for any Villainy, resolved to seize the said Snow, and murder the Captain and Mate, and, taking the Command on himself, turn Pyrate. He proposed this his Design to his Brothers in Iniquity, who approving it, he, having the Watch at one o' Clock in the Morning, on the 27th Day of <i>May</i>, went up to one <i>Morrice Cundon</i>, then at the Helm, accompanied by <i>Alexander Mitchel</i>, <i>Henry Hill</i>, <i>Samuel Cole</i>, <i>Thomas Winthrop</i>, and other Conspirators, and swore damn him, if he spoke one Word, or stirr'd either Hand or Foot, he would blow his Brains out; and, tucking up his Shirt above his Elbow, with a Cutlass in his Hand, he, with <i>Mitchel</i>, went into the Captain's Cabbin, and told him, he must turn out. The Captain asking what was the Matter, was answered, by <i>Mitchel</i>, they had no Time to answer impertinent Questions; that if he would turn out, and go upon Deck quietly, it would save 'em the Trouble of scraping the Cabbin; if he would not, a few Buckets of Water and a Scraper would take his Blood out of the Decks. That they had chosen Captain <i>Fly</i> for Commander, and damn his Blood, they would allow of no other, and would not waste their Provisions to feed useless Men.The Captain reply'd, that since they had so resolved he should make no Resistance; but begged they would not murder him, since his living could be no Obstacle to their Designs; that he had never been harsh to either of them, and therefore they could not kill him out of revenge; and if it was only for their Security, he desired, if they would not take his Word to do nothing to obstruct the Measures they had resolved on, they would secure him in Irons till he might be put somewhere on Shore. Ay, G--d d--mn ye, says <i>Fly</i>, to live and hang us, if we are ever taken: No, no, walk up and be damn'd, that Bite won't take, it has hanged many an honest Fellow already. <i>Mitchel</i> and <i>Fly</i> then laying hold of him, pulled him out of his Bed. The poor Captain intreating to spare his Life, for his Soul's sake, told 'em he would bind himself down by the most solemn Oaths, never to appear against them; that he was unfit to appear before the Judgment Seat of a just and pure God; that he was loaded with Sins, and to take him off before he had washed those Stains which sullied his Soul by the Tears of Repentance, would be a Cruelty beyond Comparison greater than that of depriving him of Life, were he prepared for Death, since it would be, without any Offence committed against them, dooming him to eternal Misery; however, if they would not be perswaded that his Life was consistent with their Safety, he begg'd they would allow him some Time to prepare himself for the great Change. That he begg'd no other Mercy than what the Justice and Compassion of the Laws would allow them, should they hereafter be taken. <i>D--n your Blood</i>, said Mitchel, <i>no Preaching. Be damn'd an you will, what's that to us? Let him look out who has the Watch. Upon Deck, you Dog, for we shall lose no more Time about you.</i>They hawl'd him into the Steerage, and forc'd him upon Deck, where one of the Hell-Hounds asked if he had rather take a Leap like a brave Fellow, or be to toss'd over like a sneaking Rascal. The Captain, addressing himself to <i>Fly</i>, said, <i>Boatswain, for God's sake don't throw me overboard, if you do, I am for ever lost; Hell's the Portion of my Crimes</i>.--Damn him answer'd <i>Fly</i>, since he's so devilish godly, we'll give him Time to say his Prayers, and I'll be Parson. Say after me. <i>Lord, have Mercy on me.</i> Short Prayers are best, so no more Words, and over with him, my Lads.

The Captain still cry'd for Mercy, and begg'd an Hour's respite only, but all in vain; he was seized by the Villains, and thrown over Board; catch'd however, and hung by the Main-Sheet, which <i>Winthorp</i> seeing, fetch'd the Cooper's broad Ax, and chopping off the unhappy Master's Hand, he was swallowed up by the Sea.

The Captain being thus dispatched, <i>Thomas Jenkins</i>, the Mate, was secured and brought upon Deck, to share the same cruel Fate. His Intreaties were as useless as the Captain's; the Sentence they had passed upon him was not to be reversed; they were deaf to his Prayers and Remonstrances, Strangers to Humanity and Compassion. He was of the Captain's Mess, they said, and they should e'en drink together; it was Pity to part good Company.

Thus they jested with his Agonies; he, however, made some Struggle, which irritating his Murderers, one of them snatched up the Ax, with which <i>Winthorp</i> had lopped off the Captain's Hand, and gave him a great Cut on the Shoulder, by missing his Head, where the Blow was aimed,and he was thrown into the Sea. He swam notwithstanding, and called out to the Doctor to throw him a Rope, who, poor Man, could not hear him, being secured, and laid in Irons in his own Cabin; and had he heard, and been able to have thrown the Rope required, could it be expected that these harden'd Wretches would have relented, and shewn him Mercy? But the sinking Man will catch at a Straw, and Hope, they say, is the last that deserts us. While we have Life we are apt to flatter our selves, some lucky Accident may favour us.

It was next debated what should be done with the Doctor. Some were for sending him to look after the Captain and Mate, but the Majority, as he was a useful Man, thought it better to keep him. All obstacles being removed, <i>Mitchel</i> saluted <i>Fly</i> Captain, and, with the rest of the Crew who had been in the Conspiracy, with some Ceremony, gave him Possession of the great Cabin.

Here a Bowl of Punch being made, <i>Morice Cundon</i> was called down, and one <i>John Fitzherbert</i> set to the Helm in his Place. At the same Time the Carpenter and <i>Thomas Streaton</i> were brought before the Captain, who told them they were three Rascals, and richly deserved to be sent after the Captain and Mate, but that they were willing to shew them Mercy, and not put them to Death in cold Blood, and he would therefore only put them in Irons, for the Security of the Ship's Crew; they were accordingly ordered out, and iron'd. <i>Fly</i> then told his Comrades it was convenient to resolve on some Course, when Word was brought them, that a Ship was very near them.The Council broke up, and made a clear Ship, when, in a very little while after, they found it was the <i>Pompey</i>, which had left <i>Jamaica</i> in Company with the Snow; the <i>Pompey</i> standing for the Snow, which did not make from her, soon haled, and asked how Captain <i>Green</i> did, and was answered by <i>Fly</i>, that he was very well. They did not think fit to attack this Ship, but returning to hold their Consultation, it was resolved to steer for <i>North Carolina</i>.

Upon their Arrival on that Coast they spied a Sloop at Anchor within the <i>Bar</i>; she was call'd the <i>John</i> and <i>Hannah</i>, and commanded by Captain <i>Fulker</i>, who thinking the Snow might want a Pilot stepp'd into his Boat with his Mate, Mr. <i>Atkinson</i>, and Mr. <i>Roan</i>, two Passengers, and a young Lad, in order to bring her in. When they came on board, they were told, that the <i>Snow</i> was come with a Cargoe from <i>Jamaica</i>; Captain <i>Fulker</i> and Mr. <i>Roan</i> were desired to walk down to the Captain, who was in the Cabbin; <i>Fly</i> received them very civilly, ordered a Bowl of Punch, and hearing Captain <i>Fulker</i> had brought another Passenger on Board, Mr. <i>Atkinson</i> was also invited down.

The Punch being brought in, Captain <i>Fly</i> told his Guest, that he was no Man to mince Matters; that he and his Comrades were Gentlemen of Fortune, and should make bold to try if Captain <i>Fulker</i>'s Sloop was a better Sailor than the <i>Snow</i>, if she was, she would prove much fitter for their Business, and they must have her: The <i>Snow</i> came to an Anchor about a League off the Sloop, and <i>Fly</i> ordered <i>Fulker</i>, with six of his own Hands, into the Boat, to bring her alongside of the <i>Snow</i>; but the Wind proving contrary, their Endeavours proved also vain, and they returned again in the Boat, bringing Captain <i>Fulker</i> back with them.As soon as they came on board the <i>Snow</i>, <i>Fly</i> fell into a violent Passion, cursing and damning <i>Fulker</i> for not bringing off the Sloop; he gave him his Reason, and said, it was impossible. <i>Damn ye</i>, replied the Pyrate, <i>you lie you Dog, but d--n my B--d, your Hide shall pay for your Roguery, and if I can't bring her off I'll burn her where she lies</i>. He then order'd Captain <i>Fulker</i> to the Geers; no Reason, no Arguments, could prevail; he was stripp'd and lash'd after a very inhuman Manner: And the Boat's Crew being sent again, with much ado carried her off as far as the Barr, where she bilged and sunk. The Pyrates then endeavoured to set what remained of her out of Water on Fire, but they could not burn her.

The <i>Snow</i> getting under Sail to look out for some Booty, <i>Fulker</i> and the others desired they might be set at Liberty, but it was denied them for the present, tho' not without a Promise that they should be released the first Vessel they took.

The fifth of <i>June</i> they left <i>Carolina</i>, and the next Day they spied a Sail, which prov'd the <i>John</i> and <i>Betty</i>, commanded by Capt. <i>Gale</i>, bound from <i>Barbadoes</i> to <i>Guiney</i>. <i>Fly</i> gave Chase, but finding the Ship wronged him, he made a Signal of Distress, hoisting his Jack at the main Top-Mast Head; but this Decoy did not hinder the Ship making the best of her Way. <i>Fly</i> continued the Chace all Night, and the Wind slackening, he came within Shot of the Ship, and fir'd several Guns at her under his black Ensign; the Ship being of no Force, and the Pyrates ready to board, the Captain struck; and <i>Fly</i> manning his Long-Boat, which carried a Pateraro in the Bow, the Crew being well armed with Pistols and Cutlashes went on Board the Prize, and sent Capt.<i>Gale</i>, after having secured his Men, Prisoner on board the <i>Snow</i>.

This Prize was of little Value to the Pyrates, who took nothing but some Sail-Cloaths and small Arms, and after two Days let her go, but took away six of his Men, setting on board Capt. <i>Fulker</i> and a Passenger (Mr. <i>Atkinson</i> was detained) and Capt. <i>Green</i>'s Surgeon; they kept this Gentleman, Mr. <i>Atkinson</i>, knowing he was a good Artist, and lately Master of the <i>Boneta</i> Brigantine, as a Pilot for the Coast of <i>New England</i>, which they were satisfied he was well acquainted with.

Upon Mr. <i>Atkinson</i>'s desiring to have his Liberty with the others, Captain <i>Fly</i> made him the following Speech: Look ye, Captain <i>Atkinson</i>, it is not that we care a T--d for your Company, G--d d--n ye; G--d d--n my Soul, not a T--d by G--d, and that's fair; but G--d d--n ye, and G--d's B--d and W--ds, if you don't act like an honest Man G--d d--n ye, and offer to play us any Rogues Tricks by G--d, and G--d sink me, but I'll blow your Brains out; G--d d--n me, if I don't. Now, Capt. <i>Atkinson</i>, you may do as you please, you may be a Son of a Whore and pilot us wrong, which, G--d d--n ye, would be a rascally Trick by G--d, because you would betray Men who trust in you; but, by the eternal J--s, you shan't live to see us hang'd. I don't love many Words, G--d d--n ye, if you have a Mind to be well used you shall, G--d's B--d; but if you will be a Villain and betray your Trust, may G--d strike me dead, and may I drink a Bowl of Brimstone and Fire with the D--l, if I don't send you head-long to H--ll, G--d d--n me; and so there needs no more Arguments by G--d, for I've told you my Mind, and here's all the Ships Crew for Witnesses, that if I do blow your Brains out, you may blame no Body but your self, G--d d--n ye.Mr. <i>Atkinson</i> answered, it was very hard he should be forced to take upon him the Pilotage, when he did not pretend to know the Coast, and that his Life should answer for any Mistake his Ignorance of the Coast might make him guilty of, and therefore begg'd he might be set on board Capt. <i>Gale</i>; and that they would trust to their own Knowledge, since he did not doubt there being better Artists on Board. No, No, replied <i>Fly</i>, that won't do by G--d, your palavring won't save your Bacon. <i>Muchas palabras no valen nada</i>, as the <i>Spaniards</i> say; so either discharge your Trust like an honest Man, for go you shan't by G--d, or I'll send you with my Service to the D--l; so no more Words, G--d d--n ye.

There was no Reply made, and they stood for the Coast of <i>New England</i>; off <i>Delaware</i>'s Bay they made a Sloop, commanded by one <i>Harris</i>, bound from <i>New York</i> to <i>Pensilvania</i>: She had on Board about fifty Passengers; <i>Fly</i> gave Chase, and coming up with her, hoisted his black Ensign, and ordered her to strike, which she immediately did; and <i>Fly</i> sent Capt. <i>Atkinson</i> on Board with three of his Hands, to sail her, tho' he would not allow him, (<i>Atkinson</i>) any Arms: They, the Pyrates, ransack'd this Prize, but not finding her of any Use to them, after a Detention of 24 Hours, they let her go, with her Men, excepting only a well made young Fellow, whose Name was <i>James Benbrooke</i>, whom they kept.

<i>Fly</i>, after having releas'd the Prize, ordered Captain <i>Atkinson</i> to carry the <i>Snow</i> into <i>Martha</i>'s Vineyard, but, he willfully miss'd this Place.<i>Fly</i> finding himself beyond <i>Nantuckets</i>, and that his Design was baulk'd, called to <i>Atkinson</i>, and told him, <i>he was a rascally Son of an envenom'd Bitch, and d--n his Blood it was a Piece of Cruelty to let such a Son of a Whore live, who design'd the Death of so many honest Fellows</i>. <i>Atkinson</i>, in his Defence said, he never pretended to know the Coast, and that it was very hard he should die for being thought an abler Man than he really was; had he pretended to be their Pilot, and did not know his Business, he deserved Punishment; but when he was forc'd upon a Business which he before declared he did not understand, it would be certainly cruel to make him suffer for their Mistake.--<i>G--d d--n ye</i>, replied <i>Fly</i>, <i>you are an obstinate Villain, and your Design is to hang us; but, B--d and W--ds you Dog, you shan't live to see it</i>, and saying this, he ran into his Cabbin and fetch'd a Pistol with Design to shoot <i>Atkinson</i>; but by the Interposition of <i>Mitchell</i>, who thought him innocent of any Design, he escaped.

<i>Atkinson</i>, who perceived his Life every Minute in Danger, began to ingratiate himself with the Pyrates, and gave them Hopes, that with good and gentle Usage, he might be brought to join them; this he did not say in express Terms, but by Words he now and then let drop, as by Accident: They were not a little rejoiced at the View of having so good an Artist to join them; nay, some of them hinted to him, that if he would take upon him the Command, they were ready to dispossess Capt. <i>Fly</i>, who carried his Command too high, and was known to all the Crew to be no Artist, and to understand nothing beyond the Business of a Boatswain. <i>Atkinson</i> thought it his Interest to keep them in the Opinion that he would join; but always declined hearing any Thing as to the Command.This made him less severely us'd, and protected him from the Insults of <i>Fly</i>, who imagined he would betray them the first Opportunity, and therefore more than once proposed his being thrown over Board, which was never approved by the <i>Snow</i>'s Company.

From <i>Nantuket</i> they stood to the Eastward, and off <i>Brown</i>'s <i>Bank</i> made a Fishing Schooner. <i>Fly</i> coming up with her fired a Gun, and hoisting his black Ensign, swore, <i>d--n his Blood, if they did not instantly bring to, and send their Boat on Board, he would sink her</i>: The Schooner obeyed, and sent away her Boat on Board the <i>Snow</i>; he examined the Captain what Vessels were to be met with, and promised, if he could put him in the Way of meeting with a good Sailor, to let him go, and give him his Vessel, or he should otherwise keep her: The poor Man told him, he had a Companion which would soon be in Sight, and was a much better Vessel; accordingly about 12 at Noon the same Day, which was the 23d of <i>June</i>, the other Schooner hove in Sight; upon which, <i>Fly</i> mann'd this Prize with six Pyrates, and a Prisoner nam'd <i>George Tasker</i>, and sent her in Chase, having himself on Board the <i>Snow</i>, no more than three Pyrates, Captain <i>Atkinson</i>, (who had work'd himself into some Favour with him) and fifteen forced Men; but he took Care to have his Arms upon Deck by him.

The Men who had not taken on with <i>Fly</i>, were, <i>Atkinson</i>, Capt. <i>Fulker</i>'s Mate, and two Youths belonging to him; the Carpenter and Gunner belonging formerly to Captain <i>Green</i>; six of Captain <i>Gate</i>'s Men, and the aforesaid <i>Benbrooke</i>, who belonged to Captain <i>Harris</i>, with three of the Men out of the Schooner.<i>Atkinson</i> seeing the Prisoners and forced Men were five to one of the Pyrates, thought of delivering himself from the Bondage he was in; and, as by good Luck, several other Fishing Vessels hove in Sight, right a-head of the <i>Snow</i>, he call'd to Captain <i>Fly</i>, and told him, he spied several other Vessels a-head, desiring, he would come forward and bring his Glass; <i>Fly</i> did so, and leaving his Arms on the Quarter-Deck, sat him on the Windlass to see if he could make what they were. <i>Atkinson</i>, who had concerted his Measures, with one <i>Walker</i> and the above-mention'd <i>Benbrooke</i>, secured the Arms on the Quarter-Deck, and gave them a Signal to seize <i>Fly</i>, which they did, with very little Trouble, and after made themselves Masters of the other three Pyrates and the <i>Snow</i>; the rest of the Prisoners, not knowing any Thing of, or what the Design might be, remaining altogether inactive, and brought the <i>Snow</i> and Pyrates to <i>Great Brewster</i>, where a Guard was put on Board <i>June</i> 28, 1726.

Soon after, the said Pyrates were brought to their Trial, that is, on the fourth of <i>July</i> following, before the Honourable <i>William Dummer</i>, Esq; Lieutenant Governor and Commander in chief, of the Province of <i>Massachuset</i>'s Bay, President of the special Court of <i>Admiralty</i>, held at the Court-house of <i>Boston</i>, assisted by 18 Gentlemen of the Council, before whom they were found guilty of Murder and Pyracy, condemn'd to be executed, and accordingly were executed the 12th of <i>July</i>; <i>Fly</i> was order'd to be hang'd in Chains at the Entrance of the Harbour of <i>Boston</i>. Thus ended the short Reign of an obdurate Wretch, who only wanted Skill and Power to be as infamous as any who had scoured the Seas; the Names of the three Pyrates, executed with him, were <i>Samuel Cole</i>, <i>George Condick</i> and <i>Henry Greenvill</i>.        OF
Capt. <i>Thomas Howard</i>,
    And his CREW.

We have said in another Life, <i>viz.</i> <i>White</i>'s, that he was a Lighterman on the River <i>Thames</i>, his Father was of the Business, and had the Character of a very honest Man. After his Father's Decease, he grew very extravagant, and squander'd away not only what he had left his Son, but what he had allotted for his Widow Mother to our Adventurer, whose Indulgence putting every Thing into her Son's Hands, was follow'd by being her self turn'd out of Doors, for he sold the House over her Head. After having ruin'd himself and Mother, his Friends fearing the Wickedness of his Inclinations would bring a Scandal upon them, persuaded him to go to Sea, and procur'd him a Voyage to <i>Jamaica</i>, on board a Merchant Ship. At this Island he ran away from his Ship, and associating himself with some desperate Fellows, they stole a Canoe, and went away to the grand <i>Camanas</i> to join some others of their own Stamp, who lurked thereabouts, with Design to go on the <i>Account</i>, the Term for Pyrating: They met those they look'd for, made up a Company of 20 Men, surprized and made themselves Masters of a Turtling Sloop, and set out in Search of Booty.

The first Prizes they made were only Turtlers, which, however, encreas'd the Number of their Crew, some being willing to join them, others being forced, with Threats of being set ashore on some desolate Key.

They after some Time cruizing met with an <i>Irish</i> Brigantine, who had Provisions and Servants on board.They made an Exchange with the Master, gave him Provision to carry him to <i>Jamaica</i>, and allow'd five Hands to go with him; the rest (except the Servants, who readily took on with the Pyrates) were all forced.

Not long after, they surprized a Sloop which had been trading on the <i>Spanish</i> Coast; as she had 6 Guns, and was a fit Vessel for their Turn, they chang'd her against the Brigantine; several Hands belonging to this Sloop enter'd Voluntiers, and several more were obliged to join them by Compulsion.

After this Capture, they steer'd for the Coast of <i>Virginia</i>, and, in their Way, met with a large <i>New England</i> Brigantine, laden with Provisions, bound for <i>Barbadoes</i>. This they made Prize of; and shifting their own Guns on board her, sent the Master away in the Sloop; after forcing some of his Men with them. They had now a Vessel of 10 Guns, and a Crew of 80 Men, of whom one <i>James</i> was Captain, and <i>Howard</i> Quarter-Master.

While they lay on the Coast of <i>Virginia</i>, they made Prize of several Ships from <i>England</i>, out of which they took Men, Liquors, Provisions, Cloaths, and whatever else they either liked or thought necessary. As these Ships had several Felons on board, who were Transports, they had out of them a Number of Voluntiers, beside forced Men; so that they had a large Complement. Among other <i>Virginia</i> Ships which fell into their Hands, they made Prize, with little Trouble, of a fine Galley, mounted with 24 Guns, which afforded them a great many Voluntiers, as she had a Number of transported Malefactors and Servants on board.They changed their Brigantine for this Ship, and soon after, the Man of War, which waited on this Coast, heaving in Sight, they thought proper to take their Departure.

From the Coast of <i>Virginia</i>, they shap'd their Course for that of <i>Guiney</i>, where they took a great many Ships of different Nations, all which they rifled of what they thought fit: Out of these Ships they forced on board a Number of Men, equal to the Number of those formerly compell'd, who desired, and whom they permitted, to be discharged, after much Entreaty.

After they had been some Months on the Coast, they spied a large three deck'd <i>Portuguese</i> Ship from <i>Brazil</i>, mounted with 36 Guns; they gave Chase and came up with her: The Captain would make no Resistance, but his Mate, who was an <i>Englishman</i>, named <i>Rutland</i>, thinking it Shame to give up such a Ship, resolved to defend her; which the <i>Portuguese</i> Captain consented to, but went himself out of Harm's Way. <i>Rutland</i>, who had been Master of an <i>English</i> Brigantine, taken from him on the same Coast by another Gang of Pyrates, fought them the better Part of a Forenoon; but the <i>Portuguese</i> flying the Decks, and only thirty Men, who were <i>English</i>, <i>Dutch</i>, and <i>French</i>, standing by him, he was obliged to ask Quarters, which were given. When the Pyrates came on board, they asked <i>Rutland</i>, if he was Commander? he answer'd, No. They enquired after him, and being told, he was somewhere in the Hold, they search'd, and found him hid in the Powder-Room; whence they hawled him up, and whipp'd him round the Deck for his Cowardice.<i>Rutland</i>, and those who fought the Ship, they forced on board, and their Complement being now 180 Men, they exchanged their Galley for the <i>Portuguese</i> Ship, carried her in Shore, and ripping off her upper Deck, made her deep wasted, and much snugger, by cutting down some of her Gunnel. This Prize they named the <i>Alexander</i>.

They went down the Coast in this Ship, and made several Prizes, some of which they discharged, and put on board such of their forced Men as begg'd their Discharge; others, they sunk, and burnt others; but forced on board all Carpenters, Cawlkers, Armorers, Surgeons, and Musicians. In their Way to <i>Cape Lopez</i>, where they designed, and afterwards did clean, they found a large <i>Bristol</i> Ship at an Anchor, which had lost a great many Men by Sickness, and had then but few healthy on board, who got into the Boat, and endeavoured to get to Shore, but were prevented by the Pyrates: Here they changed some more of their forced Men, and did intend to change their Ship; but on a Survey, found the <i>Bristol</i> Man too old for their Purpose, and therefore left her at an Anchor, after they had taken what they thought of Use to them; this Ship belong'd to one Mr. <i>Godly</i> of <i>Bristol</i>.

They met with nothing else in their Way to <i>Cape Lopez</i>, where they clean'd their Ship, took in Wood and Water, and then stood away to Sea again.

At their leaving <i>Cape Lopez</i>, they spied an <i>English</i> Ship, which they came up with and engaged; the Merchant Man made an obstinate Defence, and finding the Design to board, made to close Quarters.<i>Howard</i> and seven or eight more entered, but the Pyrate's Boatswain not having secured his lashing, they fell a-stern, and left these Men on board the Merchant-Ship, who seeing themselves in Danger, hawl'd up the Boat, which the Chace had a-stern, and, cutting the Rope, got on board the <i>Alexander</i>, which being considerably the larger Ship, and drawing a great deal more Water, stuck on an unknown Bank, which the Merchant Man went over, and by this lucky Accident escaped.

This obliged the Pyrates to start their Water, and throw over the Wood to get the Ship off, which put 'em under a Necessity of going back to <i>Cape Lopez</i> to take in those Necessaries. After having a second time wooded and water'd, they put again to Sea, fell in with and took two <i>Portuguese</i> Brigantines, which they burnt, and setting the Men on Shoar, they made for, and doubled the Cape of <i>Good Hope</i>, and shap'd their Course for <i>Madagascar</i>, where to the Northward of, and forty Mile from, the Bay of <i>Augustine</i>, and near a small Island, they run the Ship on a Reef, where she stuck fast. The Captain being then sick in his Bed, the Men went ashoar on the small adjacent Island, and carried off a great deal of Provision and Water to lighten the Ship, on board of which none but the Captain, the Quarter-Master, and about eleven more were left.

The Quarter-Master, who was <i>Howard</i>, with the others, took all the Treasure, and put it on board the Boats, made off for the Main of <i>Madagascar</i>; the Captain, hearing no Body stir upon Deck, made shift to crawl out of his Cabbin, and seeing 'em put off, fir'd the two fore chace Guns at 'em, which alarm'd (to no Purpose) the Men ashoar; as the Sea ebb'd, the Ship lay dry, and they could walk to her from the Island.She might have been saved had they had the Boats to carry out an Anchor; but for want of them they brought every Thing ashoar, at Tide of Flood, upon Rafts. As the Ship lay in a quiet Place, they had Opportunity to rip her up, and build a Vessel out of her Wreck. The major Part of the Crew being <i>English</i> and <i>Dutch</i>, who sided together, they forced about 36 <i>Portuguese</i> and <i>French</i> (thinking the Crew too numerous for their Provisions in their present Circumstances) to get upon a Raft, and take their Chance with the Sea-Breeze to get to the Island of <i>Madagascar</i>, about 3 Leagues from them. They finish'd a Vessel of 60 Tuns, but the Day they design'd to have launch'd her, a Pyrate Brigantine hove in Sight, who took 'em on board.

<i>Howard</i> and his Consorts stood along the West-Side of the Island, with Design to round the North End, and to go to St. <i>Mary</i>'s, but finding the Current too strong to stem, they lay there about a Fortnight; in the Interim they spied three Sail of tall Ships, which were Men of War under Commadore <i>Littleton</i>, (<i>viz.</i> the <i>Anglesea</i>, <i>Hastings</i> and <i>Lizard</i>,) who had carried a Pardon to the Island of St. <i>Mary</i>, accepted of by many of the Pyrates. Thinking these might be also Pyrates, they made a Smoak, which brought the Boats ashoar; but finding they were Men of War's Boats, the Pyrates thought fit to abscond, wherefore finding nothing, nor any Body, the said Men of War's Boats return'd, and the Ships kept their Cruise.

They had here plenty of Fish and wild Hogs, which they found in the Wood. One Day, when <i>Howard</i> was Hunting, his Comrades took the Opportunity, went off, rounded the North End, and left Mr. <i>Howard</i> to provide for himself.About four or five and twenty Leagues from the Cape, they went into a fine Harbour on the East Side, not frequented nay, hardly known to the <i>European</i> Ships. They were here received handsomely, treated and provided with fresh Meat, and what Necessaries they wanted, by the King of this District, whose Name was <i>Mushmango</i>, who had formerly been driven from <i>Augustine</i> by War, and travelling thro' the Heart of the Country, had here fixed his Settlement. When the Boats were victualled, and while <i>Johnson</i>, who took on him the Command after they had deserted <i>Howard</i>, was ashoar with three more, the rest went off with the Boats and Booty, and stood away to the Southward, along the Coast, designing for St. <i>Mary</i>'s, going every Night into some Harbour, or coming to an Anchor under some Point when the Winds proved contrary.

<i>Johnson</i> addressed himself to the King, and told him the Boat and Goods were his Property; upon which he went along Shore with a Number of Men, and found the Boat at an Anchor, and all asleep, except one to look out, at whom the King fired his Blunderbuss, and kill'd him; the Report of the Piece awaken'd the others, who cut and stood off the Coast. The King return'd, gave <i>Johnson</i> an Account of his Expedition, and furnish'd him a Canoe, some Calabashes of fresh Water, Provisions and Launces, that he might pursue after his People.

<i>Johnson</i> kept the Shoar on Board till he came to the Island of St. <i>Mary</i>'s, where he heard his Comrade Fugitives were gone to, and settled at <i>Ambonavoula</i>, in a Village belonging to the Natives on the River of <i>Manansallang</i>; leaving his Canoe, he went into one belonging to an Inhabitant, who carried him to his Companions.After he had been here some few Months <i>Fourgette</i>, already mention'd in <i>White</i>'s Life, came in with his Ship from <i>Martinico</i>: With this Vessel they sail'd to the West-Side, and came to an Anchor at an Island called <i>Anquawla</i>, 30 Leagues from the Place where they left <i>Howard</i>.

Some of the Subjects of the King of <i>Anquawla</i> had before met with, and brought hither, Captain <i>Howard</i>, who seeing the Ship at an Anchor near Shore, haled her, and desired the Boat might be sent to fetch him off, which was accordingly done, and he joined the rest of the Crew.

Here two Boys ran away from them, whom they demanded of the King; but he not delivering them, they went ashore by Day-Break, surpriz'd his Town, and brought off 12 of his Concubines, whom they detain'd on board, till their Boys, who were Blacks, were returned, and then delivered them back. From this Ship he went on board the <i>Speaker</i>, where he continued till she was lost on <i>Mauritius</i>, when he came back to <i>Madagascar</i>, and settled at <i>Augustine</i>, here he staid till the <i>Prosperous</i>, a Ship of 36 Guns, commanded by Captain <i>Hilliard</i>, came in; which <i>Howard</i> and some other Pyrates, (with the Assistance of the Boatswain and some of the Crew belonging to the Ship) seized. In the taking of this Ship, the Captain and his chief Mate were killed, and several others wounded. <i>Howard</i> was by the Company declared Captain.

Several of the Ship's Crew took on with them, and they went round the South End to the East Side, till they came the Length of <i>Maritan</i>, where they found some of the <i>Speaker</i>'s Company, whom they took on board, and made up their Complement about 70 Men.From hence they steer'd for the Island of St. <i>Mary</i>, where they heel'd their Ship, water'd, wooded, and shipp'd some more Hands: Here they had an Invitation from one <i>Ort Van Tyle</i>, who liv'd on the Main of <i>Madagascar</i>, to come to the Ceremony of christening two of his Children; they were kindly received and treated by him, but it having been reported, that this <i>Ort Van Tyle</i> had murdered some Pyrates, they in Revenge, tho' they had no Certainty of the Fact, took him Prisoner, plunder'd his House, and what Goods they could not take off in a great Canoe belonging to him, they threw into the River or burnt. <i>Ort Van Tyle</i> they design'd to carry on board, and hang at a Yard Arm, but one of the Pyrates help'd him to escape, and he took into the Woods, where meeting some of his Blacks, he way-laid his Canoe, and <i>Howard</i>'s Pinnace by the River Side; besides what Goods they had on board of this <i>Dutchman</i>'s, they had several Women and Children belonging to him, and some white Men, who had left them under his Care. The Pyrates set the Women to the Paddles, and the Canoe was over-set on the Bar; <i>Ort Van Tyle</i> fired on the Men, and shot one thro' the Arm and thro' the Thigh, whom with his Comrade, he took Prisoner, and kept with him: The rest of the Men got ashore on the South-Side the River and escaped him; the Women on the North-Side, and returned home. When the <i>Pinnace</i> came down, he fir'd and shot the Captain thro' the Arm, but he got on board, where his Arm was set.After this, the <i>Prosperous</i> sail'd for <i>Methelage</i>, where they victualled, with a Design to go to the <i>East-Indies</i>; while he lay here, came in a large <i>Dutch</i> Ship, well mann'd, and of 40 Guns; the <i>Prosperous</i> was not strong enough to attack her, and the <i>Dutch</i> fearing he should spoil his Trade, would not meddle with Captain <i>Howard</i>, tho' hard Words pass'd, and the <i>Dutchmen</i> threatned to fall foul on him if he did not leave the Place, which <i>Howard</i> thought fit to do, and sail'd to <i>Mayotta</i>.

Few Days after the Departure of the <i>Prosperous</i>, Captain <i>Bowen</i>, in the <i>Scotch</i> Ship, came in, anchored within small Arm-Shot, and right a-head of the <i>Dutchman</i>, whom he saluted with 11 Guns Shot and all, which the other returned, with 15 after the same Manner; Drums beating, and Trumpets sounding, on both Sides. The <i>Dutchman</i>, however, was surprized, and under Apprehensions; he hal'd the Pyrate, and answer was return'd, <i>From the Seas</i>; he then bid 'em send their Boat on Board, which accordingly went with the Quarter-Master, who told the Captain, that they had no Design on him, but were going against the <i>Moors</i>, and came in for Provision; he replied, they could get none there, and the best Way was to be gone; however the Quarter-Master went ashore (where the <i>Dutch</i> had made his Factory, and had some Goods) and shot down three Oxen, which he ordered the Natives to help to cut in Pieces; the <i>Dutchman</i> perceiving a Friendship between the Natives and Pyrates, seeing <i>Bowen</i> full of Men, and hearing two more Pyrates were expected, thought fit to go off in the Night, and leave the Goods he had put on shore.Few Days after <i>Bowen</i> seizing the Goods left, went for <i>Mayotta</i>, where he join'd the <i>Prosperous</i>, and lay for the Season to go to the <i>East-Indies</i>. After some Stay here, their Salt Provisions perishing, they return'd to <i>Madagascar</i> to revictual, <i>Bowen</i> to St. <i>Augustin</i>'s, and <i>Howard</i> (on board of whose Ship was Captain <i>Whaley</i>, taken as is said in <i>Bowen</i>'s Life) to <i>Methelage</i>, agreeing to meet at the Island of St. <i>John</i>'s, to lie for the <i>Moors</i> Fleet; where, after some Disappoints, they met, and got Sight of the <i>Moors</i> Fleet, one of which fell a Prize to <i>Bowen</i>; but the <i>Prosperous</i> being a heavy Sailor, did not come up with them till they were at an Anchor at the Bar of <i>Surat</i>, where they waited to lighten. The <i>Moors</i>, seeing few Hands on board, for <i>Howard</i> concealed his Men; and not imagining a Pyrate would venture up, they concluded him an <i>English East-India</i> Man: <i>Howard</i> clapp'd the largest on board, which stood him a smart Engagement, and killed him about 30 Men. At length the Pyrates forced Captain <i>Whaley</i>, who spoke the <i>Moors</i> Language, to go on board and offer Quarter, which they accepted: There was on board this Prize a Nobleman belonging to the Great <i>Mogul</i>, who had been at <i>Juffa</i> to buy Horses for his Master; the Prize yielded them a great Booty, tho' they found but Part of the Money which was on board. They intended to carry her to <i>Madagascar</i>, but her Bowsprit being wounded in the boarding, she lost all her Masts, wherefore, they set her a-drift, and she ran ashore at <i>Deman</i>, belonging to the <i>Portuguese</i>.From hence he steer'd to the <i>Malabar</i> Coast, where he met <i>Bowen</i> in his Prize, which mounted 56 Guns; here a Dispute arose, as shall be mention'd in the Appendix of <i>Bowen</i>'s Life, both Crews went on board <i>Bowen</i>, sunk the <i>Prosperous</i>, and burnt the <i>Scotch</i> Ship, called the <i>Speedy Return</i>: Hence they stood along the Coast of <i>India</i>, and <i>Howard</i>, with about 20 more, landed with what they had, and retired among the Natives, where <i>Howard</i> married a Woman of the Country, and being a morose ill natur'd Fellow, and using her ill, he was murder'd by her Relations.

       OF
Captain <i>LEWIS</i>.
 And his CREW.

This worthy Gentleman was an early Pyrate; we first find him a Boy on Board the Pyrate <i>Banister</i>, who was hang'd at the Yard Arm of a Man of War in sight of <i>Port-Royal</i> in <i>Jamaica</i>. This <i>Lewis</i> and another Boy were taken with him, and brought into the Island hanging by the Middle at the Mizzen-Peak. He had a great Aptitude for Languages, and spoke perfectly well that of the <i>Mosquill Indians</i>, the <i>French</i>, <i>Spanish</i> and <i>English</i>. I mention our own, because it is doubted whether he was <i>French</i> or <i>English</i>, for we cannot trace him back to his Original.

He sailed out of <i>Jamaica</i> till he was a lusty Lad, and was then taken by the <i>Spaniards</i> at the <i>Havana</i>, where he staid some Time; but at length he and six more ran away with a small Canoe, and surprized a <i>Spanish</i> Periagua, out of which two Men joined them, so that they were now nine in Company:With this Periagua they suprized a Turtleing Sloop, and forced some of the Hands to take on with them, the others they sent away in the Periagua.

He play'd at this small Game, surprising and taking Coasters and Turtlers, till with forced Men and Voluntiers he made up a Complement of 40 Men.

With these he took a large Pink built Ship, bound from <i>Jamaica</i> to the Bay of <i>Campeachy</i>, and after her several others bound to the said Bay; and having Intelligence that there lay in the Bay a fine <i>Bermudas</i> built Brigantine of 10 Guns, commanded by Captain <i>Tucker</i>; he sent the Captain of the Pink to him with a Letter, the Purport of which was, that he wanted such a Brigantine, and if he would part with her, he would pay him honestly 10000 Pieces of Eight; if he refused this, he would take Care to lie in his Way, for he was resolved, either by fair or foul Means, to have the Vessel. Captain <i>Tucker</i> having read the Letter, sent for the Masters of Vessels then lying in the Bay, and told them, after he had shewn the Letter, that if they would made him up 54 Men (for there were about 10 <i>Bermudas</i> Sloops) he would go out and fight the Pyrates. They said, No, they would not hazard their Men, they depended on their Sailing, and every one must take Care of himself as well as he could.

However, they all put to Sea together, and spied a Sail under the Land, which had a Breeze while they lay becalmed; some said he was a Turtler, others, the Pyrate, and so it proved; for it was honest Captain <i>Lewis</i>, who putting out his Oars, got in among them. Some of the Sloops had four Guns, some two, some none.<i>Joseph Dill</i> had two, which he brought on one Side, and fired smartly at the Pyrate, but unfortunately one of them split, and killed him three Men. <i>Tucker</i> called to all the Sloops to send him Men, and he would fight <i>Lewis</i>, but to no Purpose; no Body came on board him. In the mean while a Breeze sprung up, and <i>Tucker</i> trimming his Sails left them, who all fell a Prey to the Pyrate; into whom however he fired a Broadside at going off. One Sloop, whose Master I won't Name, was a very good Sailer, and was going off; but <i>Lewis</i> firing a Shot at him, brought her to, and he lay by till all the Sloops were visited and secured. Then <i>Lewis</i> sent on board him, and ordered the Master into his Sloop. As soon as he was aboard, he asked the Reason of his lying by, and betraying the Trust his Owners had reposed in him, which was doing like a Knave and Coward, and he would punish him accordingly; for, he said, you might have got off, being so much a better Sailer than my Vessel. After this Speech he fell upon him with a Rope's End, and then snatching up his Cane, drove him about the Decks without Mercy. The Master, thinking to pacify him, told him he had been out trading in that Sloop several Months, and had on board a good Quantity of Money, which was hid, and which, if he would send on board a Black belonging to the Owners, he would discover it to him.

This had not the desired Effect, but one quite contrary; for <i>Lewis</i> told him he was a Rascal and Villain for this Discovery, and, by G--d, he would pay him for betraying his Owners, and redoubled his Strokes. However, he sent and took the Money and Negroe, who was an able Sailor. He took out of his Prizes what he had occasion for, 40 able Negroe Sailors, and a white Carpenter; the largest Sloop, which was about 90 Tuns, he took for his own Use, and mounted her with 12 Guns; his Crew was now about 80 Men, Whites and Blacks.After these Captures he cruised in the Gulf of <i>Florida</i>, lying in wait for the <i>West-India</i> homeward bound Ships which took the Leeward Passage, several of which falling into his Hands were plundered by him, and released; from hence he went to the Coast of <i>Carolina</i>, where he cleaned his Sloop, and a great many Men, whom he had forced, ran away from him; however, the Natives traded with him for Rum and Sugar, and brought him all he wanted, without the Government's having any Knowledge of him, for he had got into a very private Creek; tho' he was very much on his Guard, that he might not be surprized from the Shoar.

From <i>Carolina</i> he cruized on the Coast of <i>Virginia</i>, where he took and plunder'd several Merchant Men, and forced several Men, and then return'd to the Coast of <i>Carolina</i>, where he did abundance of Mischief.

As he had now abundance of <i>French</i> on board who had entered with him, and <i>Lewis</i> hearing the <i>English</i> had a Design to Maroon them, he secured the Men he suspected, and put them in a Boat, with all the other <i>English</i>, 10 Leagues from Shoar, with only 10 Pieces of Beef, and sent them away, keeping none but <i>French</i> and Negroes; these Men, it is supposed, all perished in the Sea.

From the Coast of <i>Carolina</i> he shaped his Course for the Banks of <i>Newfoundland</i>, where he overhawled several Fishing Vessels, and then went into a commodious Harbour, where he cleaned his Sloop, and went into <i>Trinity Harbour</i> in <i>Conception Bay</i>, where there lay several Merchants, and siezed a 24 Gun Galley, called the <i>Herman</i>: The Commander, Captain <i>Beal</i>, told <i>Lewis</i>, if he would send his Quarter-Master ashoar he would furnish him with Necessaries.He being sent ashoar, a Council was held among the Masters, the Consequence of which was, the seizing the Quarter-Master, whom they carried to Captain <i>Woodes Rogers</i>; he chained him to a Sheet Anchor which was ashoar, and planted Guns at the Point, to prevent the Pyrate getting out, but to little Purpose; for the People from one of these Points firing too soon, <i>Lewis</i> quitted the Ship, and, by the Help of Oars and the Favour of the Night, got out in his Sloop, though she received many Shot in her Hull. The last Shot that was fired at the Pyrate did him considerable Damage.

He lay off and on the Harbour, swearing he would have his Quarter-Master, and intercepted two fishing Shallops, on board of one was the Captain of the Galley's Brother; he detained them, and sent Word, if his Quarter-Master did not immediately come off, he would put all his Prisoners to Death; he was sent on board him without Hesitation. <i>Lewis</i> and the Crew enquired, how he had been used? and he answered, very civilly. Its well, said the Pyrate; for had you been ill treated, I would have put all these Rascals to the Sword. They were dismiss'd, and the Captain's Brother going over the Side, the Quarter-Master stopp'd him, saying, he must drink the Gentlemens Health ashoar, in particular Captain <i>Rogers</i>', and, whispering him in the Ear, told him, if the Crew had known of his being chain'd all Night, he would have been cut in Pieces, with all his Men. After this poor Man and his Shallop's Company were gone, the Quarter-Master told the Usage he had met with, which enraged <i>Lewis</i>, and made him reproach his Quarter-Master, whose Answer was, that he did not think it just the Innocent should suffer for the Guilty.The Masters of the Merchant Men sent to Captain <i>Tudor Trevor</i>, who lay at St. <i>John</i>'s in the <i>Sheerness</i> Man of War; he immediately got under Sail, and miss'd the Pyrate but four Hours.

She kept along the Coast, and made several Prizes, <i>French</i> and <i>English</i>, and put into a Harbour where a <i>French</i> Ship lay making Fish: She was built at the latter End of the War for a Privateer, was an excellent Sailer, and mounted 24 Guns. The Commander haled him; the Pyrate answered, from <i>Jamaica</i> with Rum and Sugar. The <i>French</i> Man bid him go about his Business; that a Pyrate Sloop was on the Coast, and he might be the Rogue; if he did not immediately sheer off he would fire a Broadside into him. He went off and lay a Fortnight out at Sea, so far as not to be descry'd from Shoar, with Resolution to have the Ship. The <i>French</i> Man being on his Guard, in the mean while raised a Battery on the Shoar, which commanded the Harbour. After a Fortnight, when he was thought to be gone off, he return'd, and took two of the fishing Shallops belonging to the <i>French</i> Man, and manning them with Pyrates, they went in; one Shallop attack'd the Battery, the other surpriz'd, boarded, and carry'd the Ship, just as the Morning Star appear'd, for which Reason he gave her that Name. In the Engagement the Owner's Son was kill'd, who made the Voyage out of Curiosity only. The Ship being taken, 7 Guns were fired, which was the Signal, and the Sloop came down and lay a Long-side the Ship. The Captain told him, he suppos'd he only wanted his Liquor; but <i>Lewis</i> made Answer, he wanted his Ship, and accordingly hoisted all his Ammunition and Provision into her. When the <i>French</i> Man saw they would take away his Ship, he sold her Trim, and <i>Lewis</i> gave him the Sloop; and, excepting what he took for Provision, all the Fish he had made.Several of the <i>French</i> took on with him, who, with others, <i>English</i> and <i>French</i>, had by Force or voluntarily, made him up 200 Men.

From <i>Newfoundland</i> he steer'd for the Coast of <i>Guiney</i>, where he took a great many Ships, <i>English</i>, <i>Dutch</i>, and <i>Portuguese</i>; among these Ships was one belonging to <i>Carolina</i>, commanded by Captain <i>Smith</i>. While he was in Chace of this Vessel an Accident happen'd, which made his Men believe he dealt with the Devil; for he carried away his Fore and Main-Top Mast; and he, <i>Lewis</i>, running up the Shrouds to the Main-Top, tore off a Handful of Hair, and throwing it into the Air, used this Expression, <i>Good Devil take this till I come</i>: And, it was observed, that he came afterwards faster up with the Chace than before the Loss of his Top-Masts.

<i>Smith</i> being taken, <i>Lewis</i> used him very civilly, and gave him as much, or more in Value, than he took from him, and let him go, saying, he would come to <i>Carolina</i> when he had made Money on the Coast, and would rely on his Friendship.

They kept some Time on the Coast, when they quarrell'd among themselves, the <i>French</i> and <i>English</i>, of which the former was more numerous, and they resolved to part: The <i>French</i> therefore chose a large Sloop newly taken, thinking the Ship's Bottom, which was not Sheath'd, damaged by the Worms.

According to this Agreement they took on board what Ammunition and Provision they thought fit out of the Ship, and put off, chusing one <i>le Barre</i> Captain.As it blew hard, and the Decks were encumbered, they came to an Anchor under the Coast, to stow away their Ammunition, Goods, <i>&c.</i> <i>Lewis</i> told his Men, they were a Parcel of Rogues, and he would make 'em refund; accordingly run a Long-side his Guns, being all loaded and new primed, and ordered him to cut away his Mast, or he would sink him. <i>Le Barre</i> was obliged to obey. Then he ordered them all ashoar; they begged to have Liberty of carrying their Arms, Goods, <i>&c.</i> with 'em, but he allow'd 'em only their small Arms, and Cartridge Boxes. Then he brought the Sloop a Long-side, put every Thing on board the Ship, and sunk the Sloop.

<i>Le Barre</i> and the rest begg'd to be taken on board; however, though he denied 'em, he suffered <i>le Barre</i> and some few to come, with whom he and his Men drank plentifully. The Negroes on board <i>Lewis</i> told him, the <i>French</i> had a Plot against him. He answer'd, he could not withstand his Destiny; for the Devil told him in the great Cabin, he should be murdered that Night.

In the dead of Night came the rest of the <i>French</i> on board in Canoes, got into the Cabbin and killed <i>Lewis</i>; they fell on the Crew, but, after an Hour and Half's Dispute, the <i>French</i> were beat off, and the Quarter-Master, <i>John Cornelius</i>, an <i>Irish</i> Man, succeeded <i>Lewis</i>.

        OF
Captain <i>Cornelius</i>,
   And his CREW.Having now the Command of the <i>Morning Star</i>, <i>Cornelius</i> kept on the Coast, and made several Prizes both <i>English</i> and <i>Portuguese</i>; the former he always discharged, after he had taken what he thought fitting, but the latter he commonly burnt.

While he was thus ravaging the Coast, two <i>English</i> Ships which had slaved at <i>Whydah</i>, one of 36 Guns, and the other of 12, which fought close, were ready to sail; and having Notice of a Pyrate, who had done great Mischief, resolved to keep Company together for their Defence. The Captain of the small Ship lay sick in his Cabbin, and she was left to the Care of the Mates. When they had got under Sail, 200 Negroes jump'd over board from the larger Ships, which obliged her to bring to and get out her Boats; the Mate of the other went into the Cabbin, told the Accident, and advised lying by, and sending their Boats to assist their Consort; but the Captain being ill, and willing to get off the Coast, bid him keep on his Way, for it would be dangerous, having 400 Slaves on board, and being but weakly mann'd, when the Boats were gone they might rise upon him. The Mate urged the Danger of the Pyrates, should they leave their Consort. The Captain answered, the Seas were wide, and he would not bring to; accordingly they kept on their Way with a fresh Gale.

Two Days after, the Mate about Eight in the Morning, ordered a Man to the Mast-head, who spied a Sail, which made them prepare for an Engagement. There was on board one <i>Joseph Williams</i>, who had served the <i>African</i> Company three Years on the <i>Guiney</i> Coast, who spoke the Negroe Tongue very well;he told the Slaves he had pick'd out to the Number of 50, that the Ship in Sight he believed would fight them, and if they got the better, would certainly, as they were Cannibals, kill and eat them all, and therefore it behoved them to fight for their Lives; they had Lances and small Arms given them.

About Ten <i>Cornelius</i> came up with them, and being haled, answered, he was a Man of War, in Search of Pyrates, and bid them send their Boat on board; but they refusing to trust him, tho' he had <i>English</i> Colours and Pendent aboard, the Pyrate fired a Broadside, and they began a running Fight of about 10 Hours, in which Time the Negroes discharged their Arms so smartly, that <i>Cornelius</i> never durst attempt to board. About 8 at Night the Ship blew up abaft, they immediately cut the Lashings of the Long-Boat, but the Ship going down they had not Time to get her out, and barely enough to launch the Yawl, which lay on the Forecastle. The Ship went down on one Side, and <i>Joseph Williams</i> running on the other was hook'd by the Mizzen-Truss, and was carried down with her; but having his Knife in his Hand, and a great Presence of Mind, he cut the Wast-band of his Trowzers where he was catch'd, got clear, and swam after the Boat, into which about 16 had gotten, and either knock'd those on the Head, or cut off their Hands, who laid hold on it; however, with much Entreaty, he was permitted to lay one Hand on to ease him: They made to the Pyrate, who refused to receive them, without they would enter with him, which, to save their Lives, they all agreed to, and was then civilly received, and dry Cloaths given them; these and one Negroe were all the Souls saved.In a little Time after this he took two <i>Portuguese</i> Ships, which he plundered and kept with him; and one foggy Morning hearing the firing of Guns, which, by the distance of Time, he judg'd to be Minute Guns, as they really were, for the Death of an <i>English</i> Commander; he called his Men on board from the Prizes, sent them about their Business, and directed his Course by the Report of the Canon he had heard.

In about two Hours he spied the Ship that had fired, came up with her very soon, and took her without Resistance. The Officers of the Ship which blew up, finding this Prize <i>English</i>, and that the Pyrate did not intend to detain it, begg'd to be discharged, as they had all large Families, which must perish without their Support.

<i>Cornelius</i> taking them into Consideration, discharged Mr. <i>Powis</i> of <i>Limehouse</i>, who has since been a Commander, and raised a Fortune; the then chief Mate, Mr. <i>George Forelong</i>, the Boatswain, Carpenter, and other married Men, set them on board the Prize, and was very generous to them out of the Plunder of the <i>Portuguese</i> Ships, because they had made a broken Voyage; but <i>Joseph Williams</i> and the Batchelors he detained, and forced some out of the Prize, which he let go.

After this he took three <i>Portuguese</i> Ships at an Anchor, which he plundered and burnt, after he had hove down by one of them; he continued some Time longer on the Coast, did a great Deal of Mischief to the Trade, and forced a great many Men: These he put to do all the Slavery of the Ship, and they were beat about the Decks, without daring to strike again.I shall take Notice of an Instance of this Kind, to shew how far Revenge will carry a Man. One <i>Robert Bland</i> was at Helm, and called <i>Joseph Williams</i> to take the Whipstaff, till he went to play, <i>Williams</i> refused it; upon which <i>Bland</i> drubb'd him with the Lanyard of the Whipstaff very severely, <i>Williams</i> that he might revenge himself, and have Liberty to fight <i>Bland</i>, went that Instant and entered himself a Voluntier in the Ships Books, and ask'd Leave to fight <i>Bland</i>, which was allowed him, but with no other Weapons than his Fists; he, however, challenged his Antagonist, who was too hard for him; so that he turned Pyrate to be heartily thresh'd.

<i>Cornelius</i> thinking they had been long enough on the <i>Guiney</i> Coast, doubled the Cape, off which he spied the <i>Lizard</i> and two more Men of War, under the Command of Commadore <i>Littleton</i>; <i>Cornelius</i> was for giving Chase, but finding his Men unwilling, there being, as they gave for Reason, 70 forc'd Men on board, and these Ships being, as they suspected, Men of War, he made the best of his Way for <i>Madagascar</i>, went up the River <i>Methelage</i>, on the West Side, and anchored against <i>Pombotoque</i>, a small Village of Blacks.

The Quarter-Master went ashore, and the black Governor examined him, for several of these Blacks speak <i>English</i>; he told the Governor they were come for Provision and to trade: Upon which he sent a Couple of Oxen on board, and then ordered some of the Inhabitants to go up with the Quarter-Master to the King.The Boat's Crew seeing a Number of Blacks come upon the Strand without the Quarter-Master, apprehended some Mischief had befallen him; but were eas'd of their Fears, when they saw two Oxen given them, and were told, the white Man, who was gone to the King, would be back next Day, it not being above 20 Miles from the Shore.

When the Quarter-Master, who carried up a Blunderbuss, a fine Gun, and a Pair of Pistols, for a Present to the King, told him they wanted Provisions, he asked where they were bound? To which he answered, to seek their Fortunes, for, at present, they were very poor. Look ye, replied the King, I require nothing of you, all white Men I look upon as my Children; they help'd me to conquer this Country, and all the Cattle in it is at their Service. I will send you down Provisions enough, and when that is spent you shall have more; he accordingly sent 1000 Head of Cattle, out of which he bid them chuse what they would, and they salted up a 100 fat Oxen.

It may be an Amusement to the Reader to have a short History of this King, who was called <i>Andian Chimenatto</i>, that is, King <i>Chimenatto</i>: He was 2d Son of <i>Andian Lifouchy</i>, whose Country lay between <i>Methelage</i> and St. <i>Augustine</i>, his elder Brother was <i>Timanangarivo</i>.

At the Death of <i>Andian Lyfouchy</i>, <i>Chimenatto</i>, assisted by a younger Brother, and a great Number of the People, endeavour'd to wrest the Kingdom from his elder Brother <i>Timanangarivo</i>;but he was defeated, and with his Party obliged to retire, however, he still made War upon his Brother, till he was, by repeated Losses, very much weaken'd, and apprehensive of being attack'd by him; he retir'd farther Northward, where he made War on <i>Andian Methelage</i>, but without great Success, so that he settled on a Point of Land by the Sea-Coast, where the <i>Tyloutes</i>, that is, Inhabitants of the Sea, who are descended from the <i>Arabs</i>, and the <i>Vaujimbos</i>, who are esteem'd the meanest Cast on the whole Island, were very vexatious and troublesome to him, and kept him in continual Alarms.

In the mean while a couple of Ships arrived at <i>Yungowl</i> (the Country of <i>Timanangarivo</i>) belonging to <i>Frederick Phillips</i>, of <i>New York</i>, to slave; but hearing the old King was dead, who had a great Respect for the Whites, and that <i>Timanangarivo</i> had cut off the Crew of a Brigantine, on Pretence that they had poison'd his Father <i>Andian Lyfouchy</i>, for he drunk on board her so much Brandy that he died of it; they would not stay here, but went farther on the Coast to look for Trade.

<i>Andian Chimenatto</i> spying them, caused a Smoak to be made, which brought one of their Boats on Shoar; <i>Chimenatto</i> received the Crew very civilly, and invited the Ships in, promising Trade.

The Commanders ask'd if he had Slaves? He said he had but few; however, if they would allow some of their Men to go with him to War, he would slave both Ships: They answered, that <i>Timanangarivo</i> his Brother, had murder'd some of their Countrymen, and they could put no Confidence in him;upon which he gave them the History of his Wars, said his Brother was a wicked Man for what he had done; but if they would send some Men with him, he would give his Wives and nearest Relations for Hostages, to be kept on board.

This was agreed to, and <i>Chimenatto</i> furnished them with as much fresh Provision as they could dispose of; twenty Whites went with him to War, and they took a Town and a great Number of Slaves, out of whom he ordered the Captain to pick and cull what they pleas'd; they asked the Price, he said, he required nothing, if they would let their Men go out once more. They went on a second Expedition, took several Towns, and brought down some thousand Slaves, beside great Droves of Cattle.

The two Ships took their Choice of about 6000 Slaves, which with fresh Provision, and Provision for their Voyage, cost them only 2 or 3 Barrels of Powder, and a few Arms.

The King told them, if they would leave those Men and come again, he would again slave them for nothing; the Men being willing to stay, the Ships sail'd, came again the next Season, were slaved according to Promise, and relieved those whites, such of them as would return, and left others, who were willing, in their Steads. With this Assistance <i>Chimenatto</i> soon conquered the <i>Antylouts</i> and <i>Vaujimbos</i>, and afterwards made himself Master of the whole Country of <i>Methelage</i>, of such Reputation are the <i>Europeans</i> among these People, for they who have a white Man on their Side go on as to certain Victory; and the Sight of a white Man against them is such a Damp to their Spirits, that despairing of Success, they are preparing for Flight before they engage.But to return, besides the Present of Oxen, the King sent 100 Blacks laden with Rice. <i>Cornelius</i> sent him a Present of 2 Barrels of Powder, and would have given him more, with small Arms in Return, but he sent them Word he would have no more, nor any of their Arms, not being in Want of either; on the contrary, if they wanted he would send them ten Barrels of Powder, as they were his Children; bid them proceed on their Voyage, and if they were richer when they came back, and would send him any Present, he would accept it, but not now that they were poor.

Here <i>Cornelius</i> lost 70 Men by their Excesses, having been long without fresh Provision, the eating immoderately, drinking Toke (a Liquor made of Honey) to Excess, and being too free with the Women, they fell into violent Fevers, which carried them off.

The Blacks having given <i>Cornelius</i> an Account of the <i>Speaker</i>'s being sail'd from <i>Methelage</i> about three Months before for the <i>East-Indies</i>, he, having taken in his Provisions, steer'd the same Course, in Hopes to join in Consort with her; but the <i>Speaker</i> lying off the <i>Red Sea</i>, and the <i>Morning Star</i> going into the Gulf of <i>Persia</i> they never met: They run up a pretty Way in the Gulf, and lay under <i>Antelope</i> Island, where they kept a Look-out, and whence they made their Excursions, and took a Number of Prizes.

Here they designed to heave down and clean, and they had got a good Part of their Goods and Water Casks ashore, when the Look-out discovered two tall Ships, one of them wearing a Flag at the Foretop-Mast Head;this put them into a great Confusion, they got what Casks and Necessaries they could on board, and lay till the Ships came a-breast of them; then they got under Sail at once, their Sails being furl'd with Rope Yarns, and came close along-side the larger Ship, which was a <i>Portuguese</i> of 70 Guns, as the other was of 26; they exchanged a Broadside with her, and the smaller Ship engaged her so close, that they threw Hand Grenades into each other; but <i>Cornelius</i>'s Business was to run, and the great Ship put a Stays twice to follow him, but missing, was obliged to Ware, which gave the Pyrate a great Advantage; the small Ship in staying, tail'd aground, she, however, gave Chase till she had run a good Way a-head of her Consort, which the Pyrate seeing, brought to, and stay'd for her, as did the <i>Portuguese</i> for her Consort, not caring to engage him singly. When it was quite dark <i>Cornelius</i> ran up the other Shore, pass'd the <i>Portuguese</i> Ships (which kept down the Gulf) and came again to Anchor at his old Station, where he found his Enemies had been ashore in their Boats and staved his Casks; he here cleaned, and finding no Money to be got out of any Prizes made, and Bale Goods being of little Value to them, they from hence went away to the Island of <i>Johanna</i>, where it was designed to moroon the Blacks, who were the greater Number, and all bred among the <i>English</i>; <i>Joseph Williams</i>, fearing they would next moroon the <i>English</i>, who were not above a third of the whites, gave the Negroes Notice of the Design, who secured all the Arms of the Ship, and gave <i>Williams</i> the Command till they should get to <i>Madagascar</i>, keeping a good Guard on the <i>French</i> and <i>Dutch</i>.When they came to <i>Methelage</i> they gave the Ship to the King, her Bottom being eaten so much with the Worms, that she was no longer fit for Service; and they all went and lived with the new King <i>Chimave</i>, Son to <i>Andian Chimenatto</i>, who died before their Return: About five Months after they broke up, <i>Cornelius</i> died, and was buried with the usual Ceremony.

        OF
Capt. <i>David Williams</i>,
     And his CREW.

This Man was born in <i>Wales</i>, of very poor Parents, who bred him up to the Plough and the following of Sheep, the only Things he had any Notion of till he went to Sea. He was never esteem'd among the Pyrates as a Man of good natural Parts, perhaps, on account of his Ignorance of Letters, for, as he had no Education, he knew as little of the sailing a Ship, set aside the Business of a foremast Man, as he did of History, in which, and natural Philosophy, he was equally vers'd: He was of a morose, sour, unsociable Temper, very cholerick, and easily resented as an Affront what as brave and a more knowing Man would not think worth Notice; but he was not cruel, neither did he turn Pyrate from a wicked or avaritious Inclination, but by Necessity, and we may say, tho' he was no forced Man, he could not well avoid that Life he fell into.

When he was grown a lusty Lad he would see the World, and go seek his Fortune, as the Term is among the Country Youths, who think fit to withdraw themselves from the Subjection of their Parents;with this Whim in his Head he got to <i>Chester</i>, where he was received, and sailed on board a Coaster, till he had made himself acquainted with the Rigging, learned to knot, splice, and do the other Parts of a common Sailor's Duty; then coming to <i>London</i>, he shipp'd himself on board the <i>Mary India</i> Man, bound for <i>Bengal</i> and <i>Maderas</i>, which Voyage he performed outward, and it was not his Fault that he did not come home in the same Ship; for, in her Return, falling short of Water, they steer'd for the Island of <i>Madagascar</i>, and fell in with the East Side, in the Lat. of 20, or thereabouts. The Captain mann'd and sent ashore the Long-Boat to seek for Water, but a large Surf running, she came to an Anchor, at some little Distance from Shore, and <i>David Williams</i> with another, being both good Swimmers, stripp'd and swam off in Search of Water: While they were ashore, the Wind which blew full upon the Island and freshning, the Surf ran too high for them to get off; and the Long-Boat, after waiting some time, seeing no Possibility of getting these Men on board, weigh'd and stood for the Ship, which filled her Sails and stood for St. <i>Augustine</i>'s Bay, where she watered and proceeded on her Voyage.

Thus our poor <i>Welshman</i> and his Companion were left destitute on an Island altogether unknown to them, without Cloaths or Subsistance, but what the Fruits of the Trees offer'd. They rambled some little Time along the Coast, and were met with by the Natives, and by them carried up into the Country, where they were humanly treated, and provided with all the Necessaries of Life, tho' this was not sufficient to expel his Consort's Melancholy, who took his being left behind so much to Heart, that he sicken'd and died in a very little Time.Some time after, the Prince of the Country, who entertained <i>Williams</i>, had a Quarrel with a neighbouring King, which broke into a War. <i>Williams</i> took the Field with his Patron, but the Enemy being superior in Number, got the Victory, and took a great many Prisoners, among whom was the unfortunate <i>Welshman</i>: The King, whose Prisoner he was, treated him very kindly; and being Master of an old Musket, gave it him, saying, 'such Arms were better in the Hands of a white Man than in those of any of his Subjects, who were not so much used to them; that he should be his Friend and Companion, and should fare as well as himself, if he would assist him in his Wars.'

It will not be amiss here to take Notice, that this Island, on the East Side, is divided into a great Number of Principalities or Kingdoms, which are almost in continual War one with another; the Grounds of which are very trivial, for they will pick a Quarrel with a Neighbour, especially, if he has a Number of Cattle (in which, and Slaves, consist their Riches) on the slightest Occasion, that they may have an Opportunity of Plunder; and when a Battle or two is lost, the conquer'd makes his Peace, by delivering up such a certain Number of Bullocks and Slaves as shall be demanded by the victorious Prince. On the West Side of the Island, the Principalities are mostly reduced under one Prince, who resides near <i>Methelage</i>, and who is, as we have said in the Lives of other Pyrates, a great Friend to white Men; for his Father, who founded his Empire by the Assistance of the <i>Europeans</i>, left it in Charge with his Son, to assist them with what Necessaries they should require, and do them all friendly Offices; but if he disobeyed this Command, and should ever fall out with the white Men, or spill any of their Blood, he threaten'd to come again, turn him out of his Kingdom, and give it to his younger Brother.These Menaces had a very great Effect upon him, for he firmly believed his Father would, on his Disobedience, put them in Execution; for there is not on Earth, a Race of Men equally superstitious.

But to return to <i>Williams</i>, he lived with this Prince in great Tranquility, and was very much esteem'd by him (for Necessity taught him Complaisance) after some time, his new Patron was informed, that his vanquish'd Enemy had form'd a grand Alliance, in order to make War upon him; wherefore, he resolved to begin, and march into the Countries of the Allies, and ravage the nearest before they could join their Forces. He rais'd an Army, and accordingly march'd Southward; at the News of his Approach, the Inhabitants abandoned all the small Towns, and sending Messengers to their Friends, rais'd a considerable Body to oppose him, suffering him to over-run a great Deal of Ground without Molestation. At length being reinforced, they took their Opportunity, and setting upon him when his Men were fatigued, and his Army incumber'd with Booty, they gained a signal Victory; the King had the good Luck to get off, but <i>Williams</i> was a second Time taken Prisoner.

He was carried before the Conqueror, who, (having been an Eye-witness of his Bravery, for <i>Williams</i> kill'd a Number of his Enemies with his Shot, and behav'd very well, defending himself with the Butt End of his Musket for some Time, when he was surrounded) reach'd him his Hand, and told him, he made War with his Enemies only, that he did not esteem the White Men such, but should be glad of their Friendship.Here <i>Williams</i> was used with more Respect than he had been even by his last Patron, and lived with this Prince some Years; but a War breaking out, he was routed in a set Battle, in which <i>Williams</i> was his Companion; in the Pursuit the poor <i>Welshman</i> finding he could not get off, clapp'd his Musket at the Foot of a Tree, and climbing up, he capitulated: He was now terribly afraid of being cut to Pieces, for he had shot and wounded a great Number of the Enemy; they, however, promis'd him good Quarter, and kept their Word.

The King of <i>Maratan</i>, who took him, used him as well as any of the former had done; and carried him always with him to the Wars, in which Fortune was more propitious, for the Parties <i>Williams</i> commanded had constantly the better of their Enemies, and never returned but with great Booties of Cattle and Slaves, for all the Prisoners they take are so, till redeem'd; tho' these Prisoners are, for the most part, Women and Children, they seldom giving Quarter to any other.

The Fame of his Bravery and Success, spread it self round the Country; and his Name alone was so terrible, that the giving out he was at the Head of any Party, was giving the Enemies an Overthrow without a Battle.

This reaching the Ears of <i>Dempaino</i>, a mighty Prince who lived 200 Miles from him, and who had several Petty Princes Tributaries, he sent an Embassador to demand the White Man; but his Patron, who had no Mind to part with him, denied that he had any White Man with him, that he who was called so was a Native of the Country.For the Readers better understanding this Passage, I must inform him, that there is a Race of what they call White Men, who have been settled on <i>Madagascar</i>, Time out of Mind, and are descended from the <i>Arabs</i>; but mixing with the Negroes, have propagated a Race of Molattoes, who differ in nothing from the Manner of living of the Black Natives.

To proceed, the Embassador desired to see this Man, and <i>Williams</i> coming to him, being extremely tann'd, he had pass'd for what he was reported, had he been before apprized of what had been said, to have answered accordingly, for he spoke the Language perfectly; or had the Embassador not examined him; who, after he had some Time viewed him, ask'd of what Country he was, and whether it was true that he was one of <i>Madagascar</i>? <i>Williams</i> answered, he was an <i>Englishman</i>, and was left in the Country, relating the Particulars, as I have already set them down, adding, he had been five Years in the Island.

The Embassador then told the King, that he must send the White Man with him, for such were the Orders of his Master the great <i>Dempaino</i>, who was Lord over most of the Kings on the Side the Country where he resided; and that it would be dangerous for him to disobey the Commands of so great a Monarch.

The King answered, those who were subject to <i>Dempaino</i> ought to obey his Commands, but for him, he knew no Man greater than himself, therefore should receive Laws from none; and with this Answer dismiss'd the Ambassador; who, at his Return, reported to his Master the very Words, adding, they were delivered in a haughty Strain.<i>Dempaino</i>, who was not used to have his Commands disputed, order'd one of his Generals to march with 6000 Men, and demand the White Man, and in Case of Refusal, to denounce War, that he should send him back an Express of it, and he would follow in Person with an Army to enforce a Compliance.

These Orders were put in Execution with the greatest Dispatch and Secrecy; so that the Town was invested, before any Advice was given of the Approach of an Enemy. The General told the King, it was in his Choice to have Peace or War with his Master, since it depended on the Delivery of the White Man.

The King thus surpriz'd, was obliged, however contrary to his Inclinations, to give <i>Williams</i> up to the General, who return'd with him to <i>Dempaino</i>, without committing any Hostilities; tho' he threatned to besiege the Town, and put all but the Women and Children to the Sword, if the King of <i>Maratan</i> did not pay the Expence of his Master's sending for the White Man, which he rated at 100 Slaves, and 500 Head of Cattle, the King objected to this as a hard Condition and an unjust Imposition, but was obliged to acquiesce in it.

One Thing, remarkable enough, had like to have slipp'd me; which is, the King of <i>Maratan</i> sent <i>Williams</i> to the General without any Attendance, which made him ask, if the White Man was a Slave? The King answered, he had not used him like one. I may very well, said the General, be of a contrary Opinion, since you have sent no Body to wait upon him: Upon which Reprimand, the King sent <i>Williams</i> a Present of a Slave.He was received by <i>Dempaino</i> with a great many Caresses, was handsomely cloathed according to the Country Manner, had Slaves allotted to wait on him, and every Thing that was necessary and convenient; so that King <i>Dempaino</i> was at the Trouble of sending 6000 Men, one would think, for no other End than to shew the great Value and Esteem he had for the <i>Europeans</i>. He continued with this Prince till the Arrival of a Ship, which was some Years after his leaving <i>Maratan</i>; when the <i>Bedford</i> Galley, a Pyrate, commanded by <i>Achen Jones</i>, a <i>Welshman</i>, came on the Coast, on board of which Ship <i>Williams</i> was permitted to enter; they went to <i>Augustine</i>, where, laying the Ship on Shore, by Carelessness they broke her Back, and lost her. The Crew lived here till the Arrival of the <i>Pelican</i>, another Pyrate, mentioned in <i>North</i>'s Life; some of them went on board this Ship, and steer'd for the <i>East-Indies</i>. <i>Williams</i> shifted out of this on board the <i>Mocha</i> Frigate, a Pyrate, commanded by Captain <i>Culliford</i>, and made a Voyage; then, returning to St. <i>Mary</i>'s, they shared the Booty they had got in the Red Seas. I shall not here mention the Particulars of this last Expedition, designing to write Captain <i>Culliford</i>'s Life, which it more properly belongs to.

Some of the Crew, being <i>West Indians</i>, having an Opportunity, returned home; but <i>Williams</i> remain'd here till the Arrival and taking of Capt. <i>Forgette</i>, which has been already mentioned: He was one of those who took the <i>Speaker</i>, (the Manner has been told before, in another Life) went a Voyage in her, and returned to <i>Maratan</i>, as is said in <i>North</i>'s Life. Here the King seeing him, ask'd what Present he intended to make him for former Kindness?<i>Williams</i> answered, he had been over paid by the Prince whom he took him from and by his Services, which Answer so irritated his <i>Maratanian</i> Majesty, that he ordered him to quit his Country; and he could hardly after that see him with Patience.

From hence he went on board the <i>Prosperous</i>, Captain <i>Howard</i>, Commander, who went to St. <i>Mary</i>'s, and thence to the Main, as is said in that Pyrate's Life, and was one of the Men left behind when they had a Design to carry off <i>Ort Van Tyle</i>. This <i>Dutchman</i> kept him to hard Labour, as planting Potatoes, <i>&c.</i> in revenge for the Destruction and Havock made in his Plantations by the Crew of the <i>Prosperous</i>; he was here in the Condition of a Slave six Months, at the Expiration of which Time, he had an Opportunity (and embraced it) to run away, leaving his Consort, <i>Tho. Collins</i>, behind him, who had his Arm broke when he was taken by the <i>Dutchman</i>.

Having made his Escape from a rigid, revengeful Master, he got to a Black Prince, named <i>Rebaiharang</i>, with whom he lived half a Year; he from hence went and kept Company with one <i>John Pro</i>, another <i>Dutchman</i>, who had a small Settlement on Shore, till the Arrival of the Men of War, commanded by Commodore <i>Richards</i>, who took both <i>Pro</i> and his Guest <i>Williams</i>, put them in Irons (on board the <i>Severn</i>) till they came to <i>Johanna</i>, where the Captain of the <i>Severn</i> undertook for 2000 Dollars to go against the <i>Mohilians</i>, in which Expedition several of the Man of War's Crew were killed, and the two Pyrates made their Escape in a small Canoe to <i>Mohila</i>, where they shelter'd themselves a while in the Woods, out of which they got Provisions, and made over for <i>Johanna</i>;here they recruited themselves and went away for <i>Mayotta</i>, an Island 18 Leagues in Length. The King of this Island built them a Boat, and giving them Provisions and what Necessaries they required, they made for and arrived at <i>Madagascar</i>; where, at <i>Methelage</i>, in the Lat. of 16, 40, or thereabouts, they join'd, as has been said, Captain <i>White</i>.

Here they lay about 3 Months, then setting Fire to their Boat, they went into <i>White</i>'s, and rounding the North End came to <i>Ambonavoula</i>; here <i>Williams</i> staid till Captain <i>White</i> brought the Ship <i>Hopewell</i>, on board of which he entered before the Mast, made a Voyage to the Red Seas, towards the End of which he was chosen Quarter-Master. At their Return they touch'd at <i>Mascarenas</i> for Provisions, where almost half the Company went ashore and took up their Habitations.

From <i>Mascarenas</i> they steer'd for <i>Hopewell</i> (by some call'd <i>Hopefull</i>) Point, on <i>Madagascar</i>, where dividing their Plunder, they settled themselves.

Twelve Months after, the <i>Charles</i> Brigantine, Captain <i>Halsey</i>, came in, as is mentioned in his Life. <i>Williams</i> went on board him and made a Voyage; at their Return they came to <i>Maratan</i>, lived ashore, and assisted the King in his War against his Brother, which being ended in the Destruction of the latter, and a Pyrate lying at <i>Ambonavoula</i>, sending his Long-Boat to <i>Manangcaro</i>, within ten Leagues of <i>Maratan</i>, <i>Williams</i> and the rest went on board, and in three Months after he had been at <i>Ambonavoula</i> he was chosen Captain of the <i>Scotch</i> Ship, mentioned in <i>Halsey</i>'s Life.This Ship he work'd upon with great Earnestness, and made the <i>Scots</i> Prisoners labour hard at the fitting her up for a Voyage; and she was near ready for the Seas when a Hurricane forced her ashore, and she was wreck'd.

Some Time after this he set up and finished a Sloop, in which he and ten of his Men, design'd for <i>Mascarenas</i>, but missing the Island they went round <i>Madagascar</i>, to a Place called <i>Methelage</i>, where he laid his Vessel ashore and staid a Year; but the King being tired with his morose Temper, and he disagreeing with every Body, he was order'd to be gone, and accordingly fitting up his Vessel he put to Sea, intending to go round the North End of the Island; but the Wind being at E. S. E. and the Current setting to N. W. he put back to a Port, called the <i>Boyne</i>, within 10 Leagues of <i>Methelage</i>, in the same King's Dominions whom he had left. The Governor of this Place was descended from the <i>Arabs</i>, and it was here that the <i>Arabians</i> traded.

When he came to an Anchor, he and three of his Men (he had but 5 with him) went on Shore, paddled by two Negroes. <i>David Eaton</i> and <i>William Dawson</i>, two of the Men, required a Guide, to shew them the Way to the King's Town; the Governor order'd them one, and, at the same time, laid an Ambush for them in the Road, and caused them to be murdered.When they had left the <i>Boyn</i>, <i>Williams</i> and <i>Meyeurs</i>, a <i>Frenchman</i>, who also came ashore in the Canoe, went to buy some <i>Samsams</i>, which are agate Beads; as they were looking over these Goods, a Number of the Governor's Men came about them, seiz'd them both, and immediately dispatch'd <i>Meyeurs</i>, <i>Williams</i> they bound, and tortur'd almost a whole Day, by throwing hot Ashes on his Head and in his Face, and putting little Boys to beat him with Sticks; he offer'd the Governor 2000 Dollars for his Life, but he answer'd, he'd have both that and the Money too; and accordingly when he was near expiring, they made an End of him with their Lances.

After this barbarous Murder, the Governor thought of seizing the Sloop, on board of which were no more than two White Men, six Negroe Boys, and some Women Slaves of the same Colour; however, he thought it best to proceed by Stratagem, and therefore putting a Goat and some Calabashes of Toke on board <i>William</i>'s Canoe, with twelve Negroes arm'd, and the Sloop Negroes to paddle, he sent to surprize her. When the Canoe came pretty near the Vessel, they hal'd, and ask'd if they would let them come aboard? One of the Men ask'd <i>William</i>'s Negroes where the Captain was? He answered, drinking Toke with the Governor, and sent them Provision and Toke.A Negroe Wench advised the White Man, whose Name was <i>William Noakes</i>, not to let them come on board, for as four White Men went ashore, and none of them appear'd, she suspected some Treachery; however, on the Answer made him from the Canoe, he resolved to admit them, and giving the Wench a Kick, cryed, <i>D--m ye, must we have no fresh Provisions for your Whimsies</i>; he called them on board, and no sooner were they on Deck but one of them snatching <i>Noakes</i> his Pistol, shot him thro' the Head, and seizing the other White Man, threw him over-board and drown'd him; after which, being Masters of the Vessel, they carried her in and rifled her.

The King was at this Time a hunting, as is his Custom to hunt Boars three Months in the Year; but the Account of these Murders soon reach'd him, however, he staid the accustom'd Time of his Diversion; but when he returned home, and the Whites, who were about him, demanded Justice, he bid them be quiet, they might depend upon his doing it: He sent to the Governor of <i>Boyn</i>, and told him, he was glad that he had cut off <i>Williams</i> and his Crew, an Example he was resolved to follow and clear the Country of them all. That he had some Affairs to communicate to him, and desired he would come to Court as soon as possible, but take Care he was not seen by any of the Whites, for fear by his, they shou'd revenge the Death of their Companions.

The Governor on these Orders came away immediately, and stopp'd two Miles short, at a little Town two Miles distant from the King's, and sent Word he there waited for his Commands.The King ordered him to be with him early next Morning, before the White Men were out of their Beds; he set forward accordingly the next Day betimes, but was seiz'd on the Road by Negroes placed for that Purpose, and brought bound to the King, who, after having reproach'd him with the Barbarity of his Action, sent him to the White Men, bidding them put him to what Death they pleased; but they sent Word back, he might dispose of his Subjects Lives as he thought fit, but for their Part they would never draw a Drop of Blood of any who belonged to him. Upon which Answer the King's Uncle ordered him to be speared, and he was accordingly thrust thro' the Body with Lances. The King, after this Execution, sent to <i>Boyn</i>, and had every Thing brought which had belonged to <i>Williams</i> and his Men, and divided it among the Whites, saying, <i>He was sorry the Villain had but one Life to make Attonement for the Barbarity he had been guilty of</i>.

        OF
Capt. <i>Samuel Burgess</i>,
     And his CREW.

Capt. <i>Samuel Burgess</i> was born in <i>New-York</i>, and had a good Education; he was what they call a well-set Man, and bandy legg'd; he sail'd some Time as a Privateer in the <i>West-Indies</i>, and very often, the Gang, he was with, when the Time of their cruizing was expir'd, would make no Ceremony of prolonging the Commission by their own Authority.

By his Privateering he got together some little Money, and returned home, where the Government having no Notice, or, at least, taking none,of his pyratical Practice, in staying beyond the Date of his Commission: He went out Mate of a Ship, in the Service of <i>Frederick Phillips</i>, bound to the Island of <i>Madagascar</i>, to trade with the Pyrates, where they had the Misfortune to lose their Ship, and lived eighteen Months at <i>Augustine</i>, when an <i>English</i> Pyrate coming in, the King of the Country obliged him to go on board her, tho' much against his Inclination, for he was tired of a roving Life; but their Choice was to go or starve, for the King would keep them no longer.

He went with this free Booter to the <i>East Indies</i>, where they made several rich Prizes; returned to St. <i>Mary</i>'s, where they took in Provisions, Wood, and Water. Several of their Gang knock'd off here; but the Captain, <i>Burgess</i>, and the Remainder, went away for the <i>West Indies</i>, disposed of their Plunder on the <i>Spanish</i> Coast, and then returning to <i>New York</i>, purposely knocked the Ship on the Head at <i>Sandy Hook</i>, after they had secur'd their Money ashore.

The Government not being inform'd of their Pyracy, they lived here without Molestation, and, in a short Time, <i>Burgess</i> married a Relation of Mr. <i>Phillips</i>', who built a Ship, called the <i>Pembroke</i>, and sent him a second Time to <i>Madagascar</i>. In his Way to this Island, he went into the River of <i>Dilagou</i> on the <i>African</i> Coast, where he took in a Quantity of Elephants Teeth; and thence to <i>Augustine</i>, where he met with several of his old Ship Mates, with whom he traded for Money and Slaves. Leaving this Place he went to <i>Methelage</i>, where he also took some Money and Negroes; and from thence he shaped his Course for St.<i>Mary</i>'s, on the East Side, where he also drove a considerable Trade with his old Comrades, took several of them Passengers, who paid very generously for their Passage; and taking with him an Account of what was proper to bring in another Trip, he return'd to <i>New York</i>, without any sinister Accident: This Voyage clear'd 5,000 l. Ship and Charges paid.

His Owner encouraged by this Success, bid him chuse what Cargoe he pleas'd, and set out again; accordingly he laded with Wine, Beer, <i>&c.</i> and returning to <i>Madagascar</i> arrived at <i>Maratan</i> on the East Side, where he disposed of a great Part of his Cargoe at his own Rates. At <i>Methelage</i> he disposed of the rest, and return'd, clearing for himself and Owner 10,000 l. besides 300 Slaves he brought to <i>New York</i>.

After a short Stay at home, he set out again on the old Voyage, fell in first with <i>Methelage</i>, where he victualled and traded; from thence he went round the South End, touch'd and sold Part of his Cargoe at a large Profit, to his old Acquaintance. He made a trading Voyage round the Island, and at St. <i>Mary</i>'s met another Ship belonging to his Owner, which had Order to follow his Directions; he stay'd at this Port till he had disposed of the Cargoe of both Ships; he then shaped his Course homewards, with about 20 Pyrates Passengers, who had accepted the Pardon brought by Commadore <i>Littleton</i>.

In his Way he touch'd at the <i>Cape</i> of <i>Good Hope</i>, for Wood, Water, and fresh Provision: While he was here, the <i>Loyal Cook</i>, an <i>East India</i> Man came in, who made Prize of <i>Burgess</i>, and carried him to the <i>East Indies</i>;he there would have delivered <i>Burgess</i>'s Ship to the Governor of <i>Maderass</i>, but the Governor would have no Hand in the Affair, and told the Captain, he must answer to the <i>East-India</i> Company and <i>Burgess</i>'s Owner for what he had done.

Most of the Pyrate's Passengers thought themselves clear'd by the Act of Grace; but some of them, not willing to trust to it, got off with what Gold they could, in a <i>Dutch</i> Boat; they who trusted to the Pardon were clapp'd in Goal, and died in their Irons. I cannot omit the Simplicity of one of them, who had, however, the Wit to get off: When he designed to go away, he look'd for his Comrade for the Key of his Chest, to take his Gold with him, which amounted to Seventeen hundred Pounds; but this Comrade being ashore, he would not break open Chest, for it was Pity, he said, to spoil a good new Lock, so left his Money for the Captain of the <i>East-India</i> Man.

The News of this Capture came to the Owner before the Ship return'd, and he sued the Company; but, at their Request, staid for the Arrival of the <i>Loyal Cook</i>, which brought <i>Burgess</i> Prisoner to <i>England</i> soon after. The Captain finding himself in an Error, and that what he had done could not be justified, absconded; the Company made good the Ship and Cargo to the Owner. <i>Burgess</i> was set at Liberty, continued some Time in <i>London</i>, was impeach'd, and Pyracy sworn against him by <i>Culliford</i> (mentioned in <i>Williams</i>'s Life) who notwithstanding, he came home on the Act of Grace, was clapp'd into <i>Newgate</i>, tried and acquitted, tho' he was beggar'd.<i>Burgess</i>'s Owner stickled very hard for him, and expended great Sums of Money to save him; however, tho' he pleaded the Necessity of his going on board the Pyrate, (as his Life shews it) he was tried and condemn'd; but by the Intercession of the Bishops of <i>London</i> and <i>Canterbury</i>, was pardon'd by the Queen.

After this, he made a broken Voyage to the <i>South Sea</i>, Lieutenant to a Privateer, and returning to <i>London</i>, was out of Business a whole Year.

He then shipp'd himself Mate on board the <i>Hannah</i>, afterwards called the <i>Neptune</i>, and went to <i>Scotland</i> to take in her Cargo, the Owner being of that Nation; but before she got thither he the Owner broke, and the Ship was stopp'd, and lay 18 Months before she was dispos'd of: But being set to Sale, and six <i>Scotch</i> Gentlemen buying her, the old Officers were continued, and she proceeded on her first designed Voyage to <i>Madagascar</i>, in which the Captain and <i>Burgess</i> quarrelling, was the Loss of the Ship; for the latter, who was acquainted with the Pyrates, when they arrived at <i>Madagascar</i>, spirited them up to surprize her: The Manner how, being already set down in <i>Halsey</i>'s Life, I need not repeat.

I shall only take Notice, that Captain <i>Miller</i> being decoy'd ashore, under Pretence of being shew'd some Trees, fit for Masting, <i>Halsey</i> invited him to a Surloin of Beef, and a Bowl of Arrack Punch; he accepted the Invitation, with about 20 of the Pyrates. One <i>Emmy</i>, who had been a Waterman on the <i>Thames</i>, did not come to Table, but sat by, muffled up in a great Coat, pretending he was attack'd by the Ague, tho' he had put it on to conceal his Pistols only.After Dinner, when <i>Halsey</i> went out, as for something to entertain his Guests, (<i>Miller</i> and his Supercargo,) <i>Emmy</i> clapp'd a Pistol to the Captain's Breast, and told him, he was his Prisoner; at the same Instant, two other Pyrates enter'd the Room, with each a Blunderbuss in his Hand, and told the Captain and his Supercargoe, that no Harm should come to either, if they did not bring it upon themselves by an useless Resistance. While this past within Doors, the Wood being lined with Pyrates, all <i>Miller</i>'s Men, whom he had brought ashore to fell Timber, were secur'd, but none hurt, and all civilly treated. When they had afterwards got Possession of the Ship, in the Manner mention'd before, they set all their Prisoners at Liberty.

<i>Miller</i>, with eleven of his Men, was sent off, as is said in <i>Halsey</i>'s Life: The Company chose <i>Burgess</i> Quarter-Master, and shar'd the Booty they had made out of the <i>Scotch</i> Ship, and the <i>Greyhound</i>.

Soon after happen'd <i>Halsey</i>'s Death, who left <i>Burgess</i> Executor in Trust for his Widow and Children, with a considerable Legacy for himself, and the other Pyrates grumbling at a new Comer's being preferr'd to all of them, took from <i>Burgess</i> 3,000 l. of <i>Halsey</i>'s Money, and 1200 l. of his own, which was his Share of the two Prizes. Tho' he had been treated in this Manner, they were idle enough to give him the Command of the <i>Scots</i> Ship, and order'd him to fit her out with all Expedition, and to take on board some Men and Goods left in the Brigantine: He set to work on the Ship, with full Design to run away with her;but some Pyrates, who were in another Part of the Island, being informed of these Proceedings, thought it not prudent to trust him, so he left the Ship, and getting among his old Comrades, by their Interposition, had all his Money return'd.

After this he lived five Months on the Island of St. <i>Mary</i>'s, where his House was, by Accident, burnt down, out of which he saved nothing but his Money; he then went on board <i>David Williams</i>, when he miss'd the Island of <i>Mascarenas</i>, and returned to <i>Methelage</i>, where he staid with the King, and was one of the Men among whom he divided <i>William</i>'s Effects.

From <i>Methelage</i> he went with a Parcel of Samsams to <i>Augustine</i>, with which he bought fifty Slaves, whom he sold to the <i>Arabians</i>. In his Return to <i>Methelage</i>, he met Captain <i>North</i> in a Sloop, with thirty of <i>Miller</i>'s Men on board; these Men propos'd the taking <i>Burgess</i>, who had, they said, betrayed, ruined, and banished them their Country, by forcing them to turn Pyrates; but <i>North</i> would not consent: Upon which, they confined him, took <i>North</i> and stripp'd him of all the Money, and then releasing their Captain, gave him 300 l. as his Share, which he returned to <i>Burgess</i> on his Arrival at <i>Methelage</i>.

<i>Burgess</i> lived here two or three Years, till he was carried off by some <i>Dutchmen</i>; they belonged to an <i>East-India</i> Man, and were taken by two <i>French</i> Ships, which being bound for <i>Mocha</i>, and scarce of Provisions, came into <i>Methelage</i> to victual, where they set 80 of their Prisoners ashore. When they parted from this Port they sail'd for <i>Johanna</i>, where they left the <i>Dutch</i> Officers, who built a Ship, and came back for their Men.<i>Burgess</i> being of great Use to them, they took him on board, and steer'd for a Port, where some <i>Dutch</i>, taken in another Ship, were maroon'd; but they were wreck'd at <i>Youngoul</i>, where <i>Burgess</i> continued eighteen Months. After this Time was expir'd, he was desirous of leaving the Place, and addressing himself to the King, who was Uncle to the King of <i>Methelage</i>, he requested his black Majesty to send him back to that Port, which he readily complied with, where <i>Burgess</i> continued almost five Years, afflicted with Sickness, in which he lost one Eye. While he was here, the <i>Drake</i> Pink, of <i>London</i>, came in for Slaves, he took <i>Burgess</i>, with Design to carry him home; but Captain <i>Harvey</i> in the <i>Henry</i>, which belong'd to the same Owners, arriving, and being a Stranger to the Trade, at the Request of Captain <i>Maggot</i>, Commander of the <i>Drake</i>, and on Promise of a Ship when in the <i>West Indies</i>, he enter'd as third Mate, and continued with him. Captain <i>Harvey</i> carrying it pretty high, and disagreeing with the King, lay here 9 Months before he could slave. <i>Burgess</i> was sent up to tell the King he had not fulfill'd his Agreement with Captain <i>Harvey</i>; the King resented the being reproach'd by a Man whom he had entertained so many Years, and reviled him; he was, however, carried to Dinner with some of the principal Blacks, and drank very plentifully with them of Honey Toke, in which it is supposed he was poison'd, for he fell ill and died soon after, leaving what he had to the Care of the chief Mate, for the Use of his Wife and Children.

   APPENDIX
    TO THE
First VOLUME.We have already touched upon the History of some of the Pyrates, which we shall have Occasion to name here over again; but since the printing our first Volume, the following Relations are fallen into our Hands, which being much more curious and particular than any Thing hitherto made publick upon these Affairs, we are satisfied our Readers will find their Entertainment in the Perusal of it.

About the 20th of <i>July</i> 1718, Mr. <i>Woods Rogers</i>, Governor and Vice-Admiral of the <i>Bahama</i> Islands, being sent from <i>England</i> with the King's Proclamation, and Pardon for all Pyrates who had surrendered by a Time specified in the said Proclamation, arrived at <i>Providence</i>. It was Evening when the Fleet came off the Town of <i>Nassau</i> in the said Island, when <i>Richard Turnley</i>, the Pilot, who was on board the <i>Delicia</i>, did not judge it safe to venture over the Bar that Night, wherefore it was resolved to lie bye till Morning.

In the mean Time there came some Men on board the Fleet from off a little Island, called <i>Harbour Island</i>, adjacent to <i>Providence</i>. The Advice they brought was, that there were near a thousand Pyrates on Shore upon the Island of <i>Providence</i>, waiting for the King's Pardon, which had been long expected. The principal of their Commanders were <i>Benj. Horneygold</i>, <i>Arthur Davis</i>, <i>Jos. Burgess</i>, <i>Tho. Carter</i>, and they were all in or about the Town of <i>Nassau</i>; that the Fort was extremely out of Repair, there being only one Gun mounted, a nine Pounder, and no Accommodation for Men, but one little Hut or House, which was inhabited by an old Fellow, whom the Pyrates, in Derision, called Governor <i>Sawney</i>.The Fleet was seen from the Harbour, as well as the Town, so that Captain <i>Charles Vane</i>, who had no Design of surrendring; but, on the contrary, had fitted out his Ship with a Resolution of attempting new Adventures, took the Advantage of the Night to contrive his Escape; and though the Harbour was blocked up, and his Ship drew too much Water to get out by t'other East Passage, he shifted his Hands, and Things of most Value, into a lighter Vessel, and charging all the Guns of the Ship he quitted with Double-Round and Partridge, he set it on Fire, imagining, that some of the Ships, or their Boats, might be sent near him, and he might do some Mischief when it should burn down to them.

Those in the Fleet saw the Light, and heard the Guns, and fancied the Pyrates on Shore were making Bonfires, and firing Guns for Joy that the King's free Pardon was arrived; and Captain <i>Whitney</i>, Commander of the <i>Rose</i> Man of War, sent his Boat with his Lieutenant on Shore, which was intercepted by <i>Vane</i>, who carried the Crew on Board, and stripped them of some Stores they had in the Boat, he kept them till he got under Sail, which was till Day-break, when there was Light enough for him to see how to steer his Way through the East Passage; which was no sooner done but he hoisted a black Flag, and fired a Gun, and then let the Lieutenant and Boat's Crew depart and join the Fleet.

The Fleet got safe into the Harbour, and as soon as the Lieutenant arrived on Board, and related what had passed, the <i>Buck</i> Sloop was ordered to chace <i>Vane</i>; she made what Sail she could through the East Passage after him, having a Recruit of Men well armed sent to her from the other Ships;but being heavily laden with rich Goods, <i>Vane</i> had the Heels of her, which the Commadore observing, made a Signal for her to leave off the Chace, and return, which she did accordingly.

They immediately fell to mooring and securing their Ships, which took up the Time till Night: Next Morning the Governor went on Shore, being received at his Landing by the principal People in the Government of the Place, as <i>Thomas Walker</i>, Esq; Chief Justice, and <i>Thomas Taylor</i>, Esq; President of the Council; the Pyrate Captains, <i>Hornygold</i>, <i>Davis</i>, <i>Carter</i>, <i>Burghess</i>, <i>Currant</i>, <i>Clark</i>, with some others, drew up their Crews in two Lines, reaching from the Water-Side to the Fort, the Governor and other Officers marching between them; in the mean Time, they being under Arms, made a running Fire over his Head.

Being arrived at the Fort, his Commission was opened and read, and he was sworn in Governor of the Island, according to Form.

The next Day the Governor made out a Commission to <i>Richard Turnley</i>, the chief Pilot, to Mr. <i>Salter</i>, a Factor, and some others, to go on Board and examine all suspected Ships and Vessels in the Harbour, to take an Inventory of their several Ladings, and to secure both Ship and Cargo for the Use of the King and Company, till such Time as a Court of Admiralty could be called, that they might be lawfully cleared or condemned by proving which belonged to Pyrates, and which to fair Traders.The Day following a Court-Martial was held, in which a military Discipline was settled, in order to prevent Surprizes, both from <i>Spaniards</i> and Pyrates, till such Time as the Fort could be repaired, and put into a Condition of Defence; for this Purpose the Governor was obliged to make Use of some of the pardoned Pyrates, such as <i>Hornygold</i>, <i>Davis</i> and <i>Burghess</i>, to whom he gave some Commands; and <i>George Fetherston</i>, <i>James Bonney</i>, and <i>Dennis Macartey</i>, with some other Pyrates of a lower Rank, acted under them as inferior Officers.

Soon after the civil Government was also setled, some of the principal Officers being appointed Justices of the Peace, others of inferior Degree, Constables and Overseers of the Ways and Roads, which were overgrown with Bushes and Underwood, all about the Town of <i>Nassau</i>; so that if any Enemy had landed in the Night, they might lie in Ambuscade in those Covers, and surprize the Town; wherefore several of the common Pyrates were employed in clearing away the said Bushes and Underwood.

The Governor, with some Soldiers, guarded the Fort, and the Inhabitants, who were form'd into Train'd-Bands, took Care of the Town; but as there was no Sort of Accommodation to lodge such a Number of People, they were forced to unbend the Sails, and bring them on Shore, in order to make Tents, till they had Time to build Houses, which was done with all possible Expedition, by a Kind of Architecture altogether new.Those that were built in the Fort were done by making six little Holes in the Rock, at convenient Distances, in each of which was stuck a forked Pole; on these, from one to t'other, were placed cross Poles or Rafters, which being lathed at Top, and on the Sides, with small Sticks, were afterwards covered with <i>Palmata</i> Leaves, and then the House was finished; for they did not much trouble themselves about the Ornaments of Doors and Windows.

In the mean Time the repairing the Fort was carried on, and the Streets were ordered to be kept clean, both for Health and Conveniency, so that it began to have the Appearance of a civilized Place.

A Proclamation was published for the Encouragement of all such Persons should be willing to settle upon the Island of <i>Providence</i>, by which every Person was to have a Lot of Ground of a hundred and twenty Foot square, any where in or about the Town of <i>Nassau</i>, that was not before in the Possession of others, provided they should clear the said Ground, and build a House tenantable, by a certain Time therein limited, which might be easily done as they might have Timber for nothing. This had the Effect proposed, and a great many immediately fell to work to comply with the Conditions, in order to settle themselves there.

Many of the Pyrates were employed in the Woods in cutting down Sticks to make Pallisadoes; and all the People belonging to the Ships (Officers excepted) were obliged to work four Days in the Week upon the Fortifications, so that in a short Time a strong Entrenchment was cast round the Fort, and being well pallisadoed, it rendered the Fort tolerably strong.But it did not much suit the Inclinations of the Pyrates to be set to work; and though they had Provision sufficient, and had also a good Allowance of Wine and Brandy to each Man, yet they began to have such a hankering after their old Trade, that many of them took Opportunities of seizing Perriaguas, and other Boats, in the Night, and making their Escapes, so that in a few Months there was not many of them left.

However, when the <i>Spanish</i> War was proclaim'd, several of them return'd back again of their own Accords, being tempted with the Hopes of being employed upon the privateering Account, (which is something like pyrating;) for that Place lying near the Coast of <i>Spanish America</i>, and also not far from the Gulph of <i>Florida</i>, seemed to be a good Station for intercepting the <i>Spanish</i> Vessels going to old <i>Spain</i>.

They were not mistaken in this Supposition, for the Governor, according to the Power vested in him, did grant Commissions for privateering, and made Choice of some of the principal Pyrates who had continued upon the Island, in Obedience to the Pardon, for Commanders, as being Persons well qualified for such Employments, who made up their Crews chiefly of their scattered Companions, who were newly returned upon the Hopes of Preferment.

About this Time a Fishing Vessel belonging to the Island of <i>Providence</i> brought in the Master of a Ship and a few Sailors, whom she had picked up at Sea in a Canoe; the said Master was called Captain <i>King</i>, who sailed in a Ship called the <i>Neptune</i>, belonging to <i>South-Carolina</i>, loaden with Rice, Pitch, Tar, and other Merchandizes, bound for <i>London</i>.The Account he gave of himself was, that he was met with by <i>Charles Vane</i> the Pyrate, who carried him into <i>Green Turtle</i> Bay, one of the <i>Bahama</i> Islands, by whom he was plundered of a great Part of his Cargoe, which consisting chiefly of Stores, was of great Use to them; that afterwards they cut away Part of one of the Masts of the Ship, and fired a Gun down her Hold, with Design of sinking her; that they took some of his Men into their Service, and when they were sailing off gave him and the rest a Canoe to save themselves; that with this Canoe they made shift to sail from one little Island to another, till they had the good Luck to meet the Fishing Boat which took them up; and that he believed <i>Charles Vane</i> might still be cruising thereabouts.

Upon this Intelligence the Governor fitted out a Ship which was named the <i>Willing Mind</i>, mann'd with fifty stout Hands, well armed, and also a Sloop with thirty Hands, which he sent to cruise amongst those Islands, in search of <i>Vane</i> the Pyrate, giving them Orders also to endeavour to recover the Ship <i>Neptune</i>, which Captain <i>King</i> told them had still Goods of a considerable Value left in her.

They went out accordingly, but never saw <i>Vane</i>; however, they found the <i>Neptune</i>, which was not sunk as the Pyrates intended; for the Ball they fired into her stuck in the Ballast, without passing through, by Reason the Gun had not been sufficiently charged, and so they returned with her about the 10th of <i>November</i>; but an unlucky Accident happened to the Ship <i>Willing Mind</i>, occasioned either by the ignorance or Carelessness of the Pilot, which was bulged in going over the Bar.In the mean Time <i>Vane</i> made towards the Coast of <i>Hispaniola</i>, living riotously on Board, having store of Liquor, and plenty of fresh Provisions, such as Hogs, Goats, Sheep, Fowl, which he got upon easy Terms, for touching at a Place called <i>Isleatherer</i>, he plundered the Inhabitants of as much of their Provision as they could carry away: Here they cruised to about <i>February</i>, when, near the Windward Passage of Cape <i>Mase</i>, they met with a large rich Ship of <i>London</i>, called the <i>Kingston</i>, loaden with Bale Goods, and other rich Merchandize, and having several Passengers on Board, some <i>English</i>, and some <i>Jews</i>, besides two Women.

Towards the North-End of <i>Jamaica</i> they also met with a Turtle Sloop, bound in for that Island, on Board of which (after having first plundered her) they put the Captain of the <i>Kingston</i>, and some of his Men, and all the Passengers, except the two Women, whom they kept for their own Entertainment, contrary to the usual Practice of Pyrates, who generally sent them away, least they should occasion Contention.

The Ship <i>Kingston</i> they kept for their own Use; for now their Company being strengthened by a great many Recruits, some Voluntiers, and some forced Men out of the <i>Neptune</i> and <i>Kingston</i>, they thought they had Hands enough for two Ships; accordingly they shifted several of their Hands on Board the <i>Kingston</i>, and <i>John Rackham</i>, alias <i>Callico Jack</i>, (so called, because his Jackets and Drawers were always made of Callico) Quarter-Master to <i>Vane</i>, was unanimously chosen Captain of the <i>Kingston</i>.The Empire of these Pyrates had not been long thus divided before they had like to have fallen into a civil War amongst themselves, which must have ended in the Destruction of one of them. The fatal Occasion of the Difference betwixt these two Brother Adventurers, was this;--It happened that <i>Vane</i>'s Liquor was all out, who sending to his Brother Captain for a Supply, <i>Rackham</i> accordingly spared him what he thought fit; but it falling short of <i>Vane</i>'s Expectation, as to Quantity, he went on Board of <i>Rackham</i>'s Ship to expostulate the Matter with him, so that Words arising, <i>Rackham</i> threaten'd to shoot him thro' the Head, if he did not immediately return to his own Ship; and told him likewise, that if he did not sheer off, and part Company, he would sink him.

<i>Vane</i> thought it best to take his Advice, for he thought the other was bold enough to be as good as his Word, for he had it in his Power to be so, his Ship being the largest and strongest of the two.

Accordingly they parted, and <i>Rackham</i> made for the Island of <i>Princes</i>, and having great Quantities of rich Goods on Board, taken in the late Prizes, they were divided into Lots, and he and his Crew shared them by throwing Dice, the highest Cast being to choose first: When they had done, they packed up their Goods in Casks, and buried them on Shore in the Island of <i>Princes</i>, that they might have Room for fresh Booty. In the mean Time it happened that a Turtle Sloop belonging to <i>Jamaica</i> came in there, <i>Rackham</i> sent his Boat, and brought the Master on Board of him, and asking him several Questions, the Master informed him, that War with <i>Spain</i> had been proclaimed in <i>Jamaica</i>; and that the Time appointed by the general Pardon for Pyrates to surrender, in order to receive the Benefit thereof, was not expired.Upon this Intelligence <i>Rackham</i> and his Crew suddenly changed their Minds, and were resolved to take the Benefit of the Pardon by a speedy Surrender; wherefore, instead of using the Master ill, as the poor Man expected, they made him several Presents, desiring him to sail back to <i>Jamaica</i>, and acquaint the Governor they were willing to surrender, provided he would give his Word and Honour they should have the Benefit of the Pardon; which, as extensive as it was, they apprehended they were not intitled to, because they had run away in Defiance of it at <i>Providence</i>. They desired the Master also to return with the Governor's Answer, assuring him he should be no Loser by the Voyage.

The Master very willingly undertook the Commission, and arriving at <i>Jamaica</i>, delivered his Message to the Governor, according to his Instructions; but it happened that the Master of the <i>Kingston</i>, with his Passengers, being come to <i>Jamaica</i>, had acquainted the Governor with the Pyracies of <i>Vane</i> and <i>Rackam</i>, before the Turtler got thither, who was actually fitting out two Sloops, which were now just ready, in pursuit of them, so that the Governor was very glad to discover, by the Turtler's Message, where <i>Rackam</i> was to be found.

The two Sloops, well mann'd, accordingly sailed out, and found <i>Rackam</i> in the Station where the Turtler had described him, but altogether in Disorder, and quite unprepared, either for Sailing or for Fight, most of his Sails being on Shore erected into Tents, and his Decks lumber'd with Goods; he happen'd to be on Board himself, tho' most of his Men were ashore, and seeing the two Sloops at a Distance, bearing towards him, he observed them with his Glass, and fancied he saw on Board something like Preparations for fighting.This was what he did not expect, for he look'd for no Enemy, and while he was in Doubt and Suspence about them, they came so near that they began to fire.

He had neither Time nor Means to prepare for Defence, so that there was nothing to be done but to run into his Boat, and escape to the Shore, which he did accordingly with the few Hands he had with him, leaving the two Women on Board to be taken by the Enemy.

The Sloops seized the Ship <i>Kingston</i>, mann'd her, and brought her into <i>Jamaica</i>, having still a great part of her Cargoe left; when she arrived, the Master of her fell to examining what part of the Cargoe was lost, and what left; he searched also for his Bills of Lading and Cockets, but they were all destroyed by <i>Rackam</i>; so that the Ship being freighted by several Owners, the Master could not tell whose Property was saved, and whose lost, till he had fresh Bills of Parcels of each Owner from <i>England</i>. There was one remarkable Piece of good Luck which happened in this Affair; there were, amongst other Goods, sixty Gold Watches on Board, and thirty of Silver; the Pyrates divided the Silver Watches, but the Gold being packed up amongst some Bale Goods, were never discovered by them, and the Master, in searching, found them all safe.

In the mean Time <i>Rackam</i> and his Crew lived in the Woods, in very great Suspence what to do with themselves; they had with them Ammunition and small Arms, and also some of the Goods, such as Bales of Silk Stockings, and laced Hats, with which, it is supposed, they intended to make themselves fine; they had also two Boats and a Canoe.Being divided in their Resolutions, <i>Rackam</i> with six more determined to take one of the Boats, and make the best of their Way for the Island of <i>Providence</i>, and there claim the Benefit of the King's Pardon, which they fancied they might be intitled to, by representing, that they were carried away by <i>Vane</i>, against their Wills. Accordingly they put some Arms, Ammunition and Provision, into the best Boat, and also some of the Goods, and so set Sail. They first made the Island of <i>Pines</i>, from thence got over to the North-Side of <i>Cuba</i>, where they destroyed several <i>Spanish</i> Boats and Launces; one they took, which being a stout Sea Boat, they shifted themselves and their Cargoe into her, and sunk their own, and then stretched over to the Island of <i>Providence</i>, where they landed safely about the Middle of <i>May</i> 1719, where demanding the King's Pardon, the Governor thought fit to allow it them, and Certificates were granted to them accordingly.

Here they sold their Goods, and spent the Money merrily; when all was gone, some ingaged themselves in Privateers, and others in trading Vessels.

But <i>Rackam</i>, as Captain, having a much larger Share than any of the rest, his Money held out a little longer; but happening about this Time to come acquainted with <i>Anne Bonny</i>, that made him very extravagant. <i>Anne Bonny</i>, as has been taken Notice of in the first Volume, was married to <i>James Bonny</i>, one of the pardoned Pyrates, a likely young Fellow, and of a sober Life, considering he had been a Pyrate; but <i>Anne</i>, who was very young, soon turned a Libertine upon his Hands, so that he once surpriz'd her lying in a Hammock with another Man.<i>Rackam</i> made his Addresses to her till his Money was all spent; but as he found there was no carrying on an Amour with empty Pockets, he ingaged himself with Captain <i>Burghess</i>, lately a Pyrate, but pardoned, who had received a Commission to privateer upon the <i>Spaniards</i>. This Cruize proved successful; they took several Prizes, amongst the rest, two of considerable Value, one loaded with Cocoa Nut, and another with Sugar. They brought them into <i>Providence</i>, and found Purchasers amongst the Factors, who came from other Places for that Purpose. The Dividend was considerable, and as soon as possible disposed of: <i>Burghess</i> sailed out in Quest of new Purchase; but <i>Rackam</i>, who had nothing but <i>Anne Bonny</i> in his Head, staid behind to spend his Money, and enjoy his Mistress.

<i>Rackam</i> lived in all Manner of Luxury, spending his Money liberally upon <i>Anne Bonny</i>, who was so taken with his Generosity, that she had the Assurance to propose to her Husband to quit him, in order to cohabit with <i>John Rackam</i>; and that <i>Rackam</i> should give him a Sum of Money, in Consideration he should resign her to the said <i>Rackam</i> by a Writing in Form, and she even spoke to some Persons to witness the said Writing.

The Story made some Noise, so that the Governor hearing of it, sent for her and one <i>Anne Fulworth</i>, who came with her from <i>Carolina</i>, and pass'd for her Mother, and was privy to all her loose Behaviour, and examining them both upon it, and finding they could not deny it, he threaten'd if they proceeded further in it, to commit them both to Prison, and order them to be whipp'd, and that <i>Rackam</i>, himself, should be their Executioner.These Menaces made her promise to be very good, to live with her Husband, and to keep loose Company no more; but all this was Dissimulation, for <i>Rackam</i> and she consulting together, and finding they could not by fair Means enjoy each other's Company with Freedom, resolved to run away together, and enjoy it in Spight of all the World.

To this Purpose they plotted together to seize a Sloop which then lay in the Harbour, and <i>Rackam</i> drew some brisk young Fellows into the Conspiracy; they were of the Number of the Pyrates lately pardoned, and who, he knew, were weary of working on Shore, and long'd to be again at their old Trade.

The Sloop they made choice of was betwixt thirty and forty Tun, and one of the swiftest Sailors that ever was built of that Kind; she belong'd to one <i>John Haman</i>, who lived upon a little Island not far from <i>Providence</i>, which was inhabited by no humane Creature except himself and his Family, (for he had a Wife and Children) his Livelihood and constant Employment was to plunder and pillage the <i>Spaniards</i>, whose Sloops and Launces he had often surprized about <i>Cuba</i> and <i>Hispaniola</i>, and sometimes brought off a considerable Booty, always escaping by a good Pair of Heels, insomuch that it become a Bye-Word to say, <i>There goes</i> John Haman, <i>catch him if you can</i>. His Business to <i>Providence</i> now was to bring his Family there, in order to live and settle, being weary, perhaps, of living in that Solitude, or else apprehensive if any of the <i>Spaniards</i> should discover his Habitation, they might land, and be revenged of him for all his Pranks.<i>Anne Bonny</i> was observed to go several times on Board this Sloop; she pretended to have some Business with <i>John Haman</i>, therefore she always went when he was on Shore, for her true Errand was to discover how many Hands were aboard, and what kind of Watch they kept, and to know the Passages and Ways of the Vessel.

She discovered as much as was necessary; she found there were but two Hands on Board; that <i>John Haman</i> lay on Shore every Night: She inquired of them, Whether they watch'd? Where they lay? And ask'd many other Questions; to all which they readily answered her, as thinking she had no Design but common Curiosity.

She acquainted <i>Rackam</i> with every Particular, who resolved to lose no Time, and therefore, acquainting his Associates, who were eight in Number, they appointed an Hour for meeting at Night, which was at twelve o'Clock. They were all true to the Roguery, and <i>Anne Bonny</i> was as punctual as the most resolute, and being all well armed, they took a Boat and rowed to the Sloop, which was very near the Shore.

The Night seemed to favour the Attempt, for it was both dark and rainy. As soon as they got on Board, <i>Anne Bonny</i>, having a drawn Sword in one Hand and a Pistol in the other, attended by one of the Men, went strait to the Cabin where the two Fellows lay who belonged to the Sloop; the Noise waked them, which she observing, swore, that if they pretended to resist, or make a Noise, she would blow out their Brains, (that was the Term she used.)In the mean Time <i>Rackam</i> and the rest were busy heaving in the Cables, one of which they soon got up, and, for Expedition sake, they slipped the other, and so drove down the Harbour: They passed pretty near the Fort, which hailed them, as did also the Guardship, asking them where they were going; they answered, their Cable had parted, and that they had nothing but a Grappling on Board, which would not hold them. Immediately after which they put out a small Sail, just to give them steerage Way. When they came to the Harbour's Mouth, and thought they could not be seen by any of the Ships, because of the Darkness of the Night, they hoisted all the Sail they had, and stood to Sea; then calling up the two Men, they asked them if they would be of their Party; but finding them not inclined, they gave them a Boat to row themselves ashore, ordering them to give their Service to <i>Haman</i>, and to tell him, they would send him his Sloop again when they had done with it.

<i>Rackam</i> and <i>Anne Bonny</i>, both bore a great Spleen to one <i>Richard Turnley</i>, whom <i>Anne</i> had ask'd to be a Witness to the Writing, which <i>James Bonny</i>, her Husband, was to give to <i>Rackam</i>, by which she was to be resigned to him; <i>Turnley</i> refused his Hand upon that Occasion, and was the Person who acquainted the Governor with the Story, for which they vowed Revenge against him. He was gone from <i>Providence</i> a turtling before they made their Escape, and they knowing what Island he was upon, made to the Place. They saw the Sloop about a League from the Shore a fishing, and went aboard with six Hands; but <i>Turnley</i>, with his Boy, by good Luck, happened to be ashore salting some wild Hogs they killed the Day before; they inquired for him, and hearing where he was, rowed ashore in Search of him.<i>Turnley</i> from the Land saw the Sloop boarded, and observed the Men afterwards making for the Shore, and being apprehensive of Pyrates, which are very common in those Parts, he, with his Boy, fled into a neighbouring Wood. The Surf was very great, so that they could not bring the Boat to Shore; they waded up to the Arm-Pits, and <i>Turnley</i>, peeping through the Trees, saw them bring Arms on Shore: Upon the whole, not liking their Appearance, he, with his Boy, lay snug in the Bushes.

When they had looked about and could not see him, they hollow'd, and call'd him by his Name; but he not appearing, they thought it Time lost to look for him in such a Wilderness, and therefore they returned to their Boat, but rowed again back to the Sloop, and took away the Sails, and several other Things. They also carried away with them three of the Hands, <i>viz.</i> <i>Richard Connor</i> the Mate, <i>John Davis</i>, and <i>John Howel</i>, but rejected <i>David Soward</i> the fourth Hand, tho' he had been an old experienced Pyrate, because he was lame, and disabled by a Wound he had formerly received.

When they had done thus much, they cut down the Main-Mast, and towing the Vessel into deep Water, sunk her, having first put <i>David Soward</i> into a Boat to shift for himself; he made Shift to get ashore, and after some Time, having found out <i>Turnley</i>, he told him, that <i>Rackam</i> and <i>Mary Stead</i> were determined, if they could have found him, to have whipp'd him to Death, as he heard them vow with many bitter Oaths and Imprecations; for whipping was the Punishment the Governor had threatened her with by his Information.From thence they stretch'd over to the <i>Bury Islands</i>, plundering all the Sloops they met, and strengthening their Company with several additional Hands, and so went on till they were taken and executed at <i>Port Royal</i>, as has been told in the first Volume.

About this Time, the Governor, in Conjunction with some Factors then residing at <i>Providence</i>, thought fit to freight some Vessels for a trading Voyage; accordingly the <i>Batchelor</i>'s <i>Adventure</i>, a Schooner, Captain <i>Henry White</i>, Commander; the <i>Lancaster Sloop</i>, Captain <i>William Greenway</i>, Commander; the <i>May Sloop</i>, Captain <i>John Augur</i>, Commander, of which last <i>David Soward</i> was Owner, (it having been given him by some Pyrates his former Associates) in which he also sail'd this Voyage, were fitted out with a Cargo of Goods and Merchandizes, bound for <i>Port Prince</i> on the Island of <i>Cuba</i>.

The Governor thought it adviseable, for the Benefit of the Inhabitants of <i>Providence</i>, to settle a Correspondence with some Merchants at <i>Port Prince</i>, first, in order to procure fresh Provisions, there being scarce any upon the Island at the Governor's first Arrival; and there being at <i>Port Prince</i> great Plenty of Cows and Hogs, he proposed to get a sufficient Number of each, to stock the Island for Breed, that the People for the future might have fresh Provision of their own.

They set Sail on <i>Sunday</i> the fifth of <i>October</i>, 1718; the next Day they arrived at an Island known by the Name of <i>Green Key</i>, lying South, South-East from <i>Providence</i>, in the Degree of 23 and 40 North Latitude, being distance about twenty five Leagues;they cast Anchor, in order to wait for Morning to carry them thro' some Rocks and Shoals which lay in their Way, and some went ashore to try to kill something for Supper, before it should be dark; they expected to meet some wild Hogs, for, some Time before, one <i>Joseph Bay</i> and one <i>Sims</i>, put two Sows and a Boar into the said Island; for they living at that Time at <i>Providence</i>, and being continually visited by Pyrates, were always plundered of all their fresh Provisions; wherefore, they thought of settling a Breed upon <i>Green Key</i>, that they might have Recourse to in Time of Necessity.

This Island is about nine Miles in Circumference, and about three Miles broad in the widest Place; it is overgrown with wild Cabbage and Palmuta Trees, and great Variety of other Herbs and Fruits, so that there is Plenty of Food for the Nourishment of such Animals; but the Trees lying so close together, makes it but bad Hunting, so that they kill'd but one Hog, which, however was of a monstrous Size.

The Hunters returned on Board their Ships again before Seven, having first divided the Hog, and sent part on Board each Vessel for Supper that Night. After Supper, Captain <i>Greenway</i> and Captain <i>White</i> came on Board of Captain <i>Augur</i>'s Sloop, in order to consult together, what Time to sail, and being all of Opinion, that if they weigh'd Anchor betwixt the Hours of ten and eleven, it would be Day before they would come up with the Shoals; they agreed upon that Hour for setting Sail, and so return'd to their own Vessels.
